\title{Capture window and robustness of nonlinear-kicker injection into a 4\,GeV storage ring with a small dynamic aperture}

\author[a,1]{Chong Shik Park\note{Corresponding author.}}

\affiliation[a]{Department of Accelerator Science and Center for Accelerator Research, Korea University,\\Sejong 30019, Republic of Korea}

\emailAdd{kuphy@korea.ac.kr}

\abstract{
A single nonlinear kicker magnet (NKM) can inject off-axis without disturbing the stored beam, but its kick varies across the injected bunch, and fourth-generation storage rings offer little dynamic aperture to absorb the resulting spread. We quantify this trade-off for a wire-based NKM in a 4\,GeV ring whose on-momentum horizontal dynamic aperture at the injection point extends from $\DAxMinMm$ to $+\DAxMaxMm$\,mm, using the RADIA kick map, a transfer line tracked particle by particle from the booster, a 2\,m septum-to-kicker drift and native nonlinear ring tracking. A linear one-turn-map model overestimates capture by tens of percentage points. Capture is highest when the incoming angle is cancelled close to the kick-map extremum, where the kick gradient across the bunch is smallest: at the selected operating point \RepFieldmapCapture\,\% of the injected particles survive 500 turns (\ThickRematchedCapture\,\% with a thick-magnet model), compared with \RepDipoleCapture\,\% for a uniform kick of equal strength, and the angular window is about $\pm0.5$\,mrad. For an aligned kicker the stored-beam centroid moves by less than $10^{-5}$ of its rms size; because the field is cubic near the axis, a 0.2\,mm offset between kicker and stored orbit raises it to \StoredTwoTenthAmp\ of the rms size. Under a combined error budget the mean capture drops to \RobMeanCapture\,\% (95\,\% interval \RobMeanCaptureLo--\RobMeanCaptureHi\,\%), limited by the injected-orbit error at the septum that booster extraction jitter produces; correcting the static trajectory does not help (\RobCorrectedMean\,\%), whereas removing that jitter raises it to \RobNoJitterMean\,\%.
}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Top-up injection into low-emittance storage rings requires that the stored beam is not disturbed while charge is added. A conventional four-kicker orbit bump leaves residual oscillations of the stored beam because the kicker pulses cannot be matched perfectly. A single pulsed multipole magnet whose field vanishes on the stored-beam axis avoids this problem: pulsed sextupole injection was demonstrated at the KEK Photon Factory~\cite{Takaki2010}, a non-linear kicker (NLK) with zero central field and an off-axis field maximum was developed for BESSY~II~\cite{Atkinson2011,Gora2023}, and a multipole injection kicker (MIK) installed at MAX~IV and SOLEIL reduces the stored-beam perturbation below 2\,\% of its rms size~\cite{Ollier2023}.

The price of transparency is a kick that varies across the injected bunch. Liu \emph{et al.}~\cite{Liu2016} identified the nonlinearity of the kick at the injected-beam position, together with the injected-beam size, as the main cause of reduced injection efficiency. The effect is aggravated in fourth-generation lattices, whose strong sextupoles restrict the dynamic aperture (DA) available for the kicked bunch, and beam-based studies at BESSY~II reached up to 97\,\% efficiency only with optimised sextupole settings~\cite{Gora2023}.

This article quantifies that trade-off for a specific case: a wire-based NKM computed with RADIA~\cite{Chubar1998}, placed 2\,m downstream of the injection septum of a 4\,GeV storage ring whose on-momentum horizontal DA at the injection point is only about 12\,mm. We ask how wide the injection window is, how much capture is lost relative to a uniform kick, how transparent the NKM is for the stored beam, and how robust the window is against realistic errors. The injected beam is tracked particle by particle from the booster through a matched transfer line (BTS), and capture is evaluated with native nonlinear ring tracking. The study is simulation-only; it does not claim experimental or operational validation.

\section{System and model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Storage ring and dynamic aperture}

The ring lattice (circumference 799.30\,m, 3483 elements) is tracked with the Accelerator Toolbox~\cite{Terebilo2001,Rogers2017} in six dimensions with RF and radiation pass methods. At the kicker centre, which is the injection, kick and observation point, $\beta_x = 16.27$\,m, $\alpha_x=-0.145$, $\beta_y = 5.60$\,m and $D_x = 0.3$\,mm. The tunes are 68.18/23.26, the chromaticities $+6.0/+3.4$, the equilibrium emittance 61.6\,pm\,rad and the rms energy spread $1.26\times10^{-3}$. The synchrotron period is about 290 turns and the horizontal damping time about 3950 turns. The lattice contains no physical apertures; losses are produced by the septum blade and by dynamical instability.

Figure~\ref{fig:da} shows the DA obtained by native tracking of single particles over 500 turns. On momentum, particles with $x'=0$ at the kicker centre are stable between $\DAxMinMm$ and $+\DAxMaxMm$\,mm (1\,mm grid); the stable range is $\pm14$\,mm at $\delta=-1\,\%$ and shrinks to $\pm3$\,mm at $\delta=-3\,\%$. The corresponding horizontal acceptance is about 9\,$\mu$m\,rad.

\subsection{Nonlinear kicker field map}

The NKM is formed by four current filaments of length 525\,mm. RADIA field integrals along the beam axis, $\int B\,\mathrm{d}l$, divided by the magnetic rigidity $B\rho=13.343$\,T\,m at 4\,GeV, give the kick map on a $201\times201$ grid over $|x|,|y|\le50$\,mm (0.5\,mm spacing). On the midplane the integrated kick equals the central field profile $B_y(x)$ multiplied by the magnet length to within $10^{-10}$ relative, the field vanishes on axis (kick $<10^{-12}$\,rad) and grows as $x^3$ near the axis. The horizontal kick reaches its extremum of \KickPeakMrad\,mrad at $x=\KickPeakXmm$\,mm (figure~\ref{fig:kick}). The kick map carries no charge sign; the polarity is a choice of coil current. We use the polarity for which the kick at $x<0$ is negative, so that it cancels the positive angle of a beam travelling from the septum towards the axis. A field scale $S$ multiplies the map. Coordinates outside the map are counted as losses; the map is never extrapolated.

\subsection{Injection geometry and transfer-line handoff}

The injected bunch is generated at the booster extraction point with $\beta_{x,y}=7.56/12.27$\,m, geometric emittances 100/10\,nm\,rad and rms energy spread $1.1\times10^{-3}$, and is tracked element by element through the 21.79\,m BTS line with the matched quadrupole settings (exit mismatch $\mathcal{M}_{x,y}=0$, peak $\beta_{x,y}=31.4/50.7$\,m) or the baseline settings. At the septum exit the BTS distribution is shifted to the ring frame with centroid $(x_\mathrm{inj}, x'_\mathrm{inj})$. The septum blade has its stored-beam edge at $-16$\,mm and a thickness of 2\,mm; it removes injected particles on the wrong side at creation and circulating particles with $x<-16$\,mm on every turn. The bunch drifts 2.26\,m to the NKM centre, receives one thin kick (single-turn pulse) and is tracked for 500 turns. Capture is the fraction of particles alive after 500 turns, with 95\,\% Wilson intervals~\cite{Wilson1927}. For each $(x_\mathrm{inj}, S)$ the matched angle cancels the kick at the nominal centroid; the angle offset from this value is scanned separately.

Two controls receive the identical injected particles. The \emph{dipole} control is a uniform kick equal to the NKM kick at the nominal centroid, and the \emph{linearised} control is the first-order Taylor expansion of the map at that point. The stored-beam response is evaluated from the exact kicks applied to a $10^5$-particle equilibrium beam: the centroid betatron amplitude $A=\beta_x|\langle\Delta x'\rangle|$ is reported relative to the rms beam size $\sigma_x=31.7\,\mu$m.

\section{Model validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Native element-by-element tracking is the reference. A linear one-turn map with the same septum check reproduces small-amplitude orbits (relative residual below 2\,\% at 1\,mm after 10 turns) but has no DA. On identical injected bunches it predicts \ProvCaptureMap\,\% capture where native tracking gives \ProvCaptureNative\,\% (figure~\ref{fig:validation}, provisional point $x_\mathrm{inj}=-20$\,mm, $x'_\mathrm{inj}=5.75$\,mrad, $S=1$), and for the dipole control the difference exceeds 40 percentage points. The linear map is therefore used only as a cross-check. Capture converges in the number of turns: 0.44 after 500 and 0.43 after 2000 turns. Increasing the particle number from 200 to 1000 changes the estimate within its interval. With zero field scale the NKM reproduces the kicker-off case exactly. A uniform 1\,$\mu$rad kick on the stored beam gives a tracked centroid amplitude of 15.7\,$\mu$m versus $\beta_x\Delta x'=16.3\,\mu$m, which validates the stored-beam estimate. A chamber aperture of $\pm12\times\pm6$\,mm at every element lowers capture by about 3 percentage points.

\section{Injection operating window}
\label{sec:window}

Figure~\ref{fig:window} maps the field-map capture over $x_\mathrm{inj}$, $S$ and the angle offset (200 particles per point, coupled matched-BTS bunch, native tracking). With the kicker off no particle is captured at any point, because the incoming angle of 4.9--6.2\,mrad carries the bunch outside the DA. Capture is highest when the matched centroid lies close to the kick-map extremum: the selected operating point $x_\mathrm{inj}=\OpXinjMm$\,mm, $S=\OpScale$, $x'_\mathrm{inj}=\OpXpMrad$\,mrad (maximum lower confidence bound) captures \OpCaptureGrid\,\%. Increasing $S$ moves the matched centroid towards the axis, where the kick gradient across the 0.7\,mm wide bunch is larger, and capture decreases monotonically. The angular window is narrow and asymmetric: an offset of $+0.5$\,mrad reduces capture to 13--64\,\%, $-0.5$\,mrad to 15--85\,\%.

Figure~\ref{fig:controls} compares the models on four independent 500-particle bunches at the operating point. The NKM captures \RepFieldmapCapture\,\% on average, the linearised control \RepLinearCapture\,\% and the uniform dipole \RepDipoleCapture\,\%; the kicker-off case captures \RepOffCapture\,\%. The capture deficit of the NKM relative to the uniform kick is the cost of the kick variation across the bunch. In return, the aligned NKM displaces the stored-beam centroid by \StoredAlignedAmp\ rms sizes and increases its emittance by a fraction \StoredAlignedEmit, whereas the dipole and linearised controls displace it by \StoredDipoleAmp\ and \StoredLinearAmp\ rms sizes. Because the field grows as $x^3$ near the axis, the disturbance depends on the offset between the kicker centre and the stored orbit: \StoredTwoTenthAmp\ rms sizes at 0.2\,mm and \StoredHalfMmAmp\ at 0.5\,mm. The stored-beam response uses a bicubic spline through the kick-map nodes; bilinear interpolation between the nodes at 0 and 0.5\,mm would overstate the near-axis kick. With the baseline BTS optics the same NKM setting captures only \BaselineBTSCapture\,\%, so matching the transfer line is a prerequisite. The thin-kick approximation of the 0.525\,m magnet was checked by integrating the same bunches through the field profile in 160 drift--kick--drift slices: the thick magnet gives \ThickSameCapture\,\% at the same injection angle and \ThickRematchedCapture\,\% when the angle is re-matched (\ThickRematchedXpMrad\,mrad), because the trajectory inside the magnet samples a steeper part of the kick profile. We therefore regard the thin-kick capture values as optimistic by about 4 percentage points.

\section{Robustness}
\label{sec:robustness}

\subsection{Transfer-line optics}

With the matched BTS optics fixed (figure~\ref{fig:bts}), three independent ensembles of 50\,000 error realizations (quadrupole gradient 0.1\,\%, offsets 100\,$\mu$m, roll 0.5\,mrad, energy 0.1\,\%, booster $\beta$ mismatch 5\,\%) give a median horizontal exit mismatch of $\mathcal{M}_x=\TolMedianMx\times10^{-3}$ (pooled 95\,\% interval \TolMedianMxLo--\TolMedianMxHi$\times10^{-3}$). Only \TolFailures\ of the \TolN\ realizations exceeds the 60\,m $\beta$ limit, corresponding to a failure probability below \TolFailUpper\ (exact 95\,\% bound~\cite{Clopper1934}). The booster $\beta$ mismatch dominates the one-at-a-time ranking, followed by the quadrupole gradient and energy errors. A predeclared prefix-stability target of $10^{-5}$ on the median was not met (changes of 1.1--2.1$\times10^{-5}$ between 25\,000 and 50\,000 samples); the established precision of the median is about 1.2\,\%.

\subsection{Injection capture under combined errors}

Capture robustness is evaluated by tracking, for each error realization, a 200-particle booster bunch through the perturbed BTS (gradient, offset, roll and dipole-field errors; booster centroid jitter 0.5\,mm/0.2\,mrad, $\beta$ mismatch 5\,\%, energy offset 0.1\,\%, emittance 10\,\% and energy-spread variation) into the ring with septum position (0.1\,mm), NKM field scale (0.5\,\%), NKM alignment (0.2\,mm) and closed-orbit (0.2\,mm) errors, for 500 turns. Realizations are the resampling unit. Over \RobNReal\ realizations from three independent seeds the mean capture is \RobMeanCapture\,\% (95\,\% cluster-bootstrap interval \RobMeanCaptureLo--\RobMeanCaptureHi\,\%, median \RobMedianCapture\,\%) and \RobFracAbove\,\% of the realizations exceed \RobThreshold\,\% capture (figure~\ref{fig:robust}). The realization without errors captures \RobZeroError\,\%. The paired uniform-dipole control also degrades, from \RepDipoleCapture\ to \RobDipoleMean\,\% (seed 42), so most of the loss is caused by the errors; the NKM loses a further \RobPairedDiff\ percentage points relative to the dipole on identical beams, because its kick gradient amplifies the centroid errors. The injected-orbit error at the septum is the strongest predictor (Spearman $\rho=\RobTopRho$ for the angle error): the BTS turns the assumed booster jitter into rms errors of \RobExitRmsXmm\,mm and \RobExitRmsXpMrad\,mrad at the septum, comparable to the angular window. Two diagnostic ensembles on the same error realizations separate static and shot-to-shot sources. In operation the static trajectory error that 0.1\,mm quadrupole offsets produce at the septum (\RobStaticRmsXmm\,mm rms) is removed by steering; with ideal correction of the static trajectory at the septum exit, keeping the booster centroid and energy jitter and all optics, kicker and ring errors, the mean capture is \RobCorrectedMean\,\% (median \RobCorrectedMedian\,\%, \RobCorrectedN\ realizations), not significantly different from the uncorrected case. Removing the booster jitter instead, while leaving the static errors uncorrected, raises the mean capture to \RobNoJitterMean\,\% (\RobNoJitterN\ realizations). The shot-to-shot injected-orbit jitter is therefore the limiting error. Under the same errors the stored-beam centroid amplitude is \RobStoredMedian\ rms sizes (median), \RobStoredPNinetyFive\ (95th percentile) and at most \RobStoredMax, dominated by the kicker and closed-orbit offsets. Tripling all error amplitudes reduces capture to a few per cent, which confirms that the ensemble responds to the errors.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support four statements for this lattice. First, the dynamic aperture, not the kick strength, limits NKM injection, and a linear ring model cannot predict capture. Second, the useful window is defined by cancelling the incoming angle close to the kick-map extremum, where the kick gradient across the bunch is smallest; this quantifies, for this design, the efficiency mechanism identified in~\cite{Liu2016}. Third, the NKM is transparent for the stored beam, but because its field is cubic near the axis the transparency is set by the alignment of the kicker centre to the stored orbit: a 0.2\,mm offset already produces a centroid oscillation of about a tenth of the rms beam size. Fourth, with an angular window of about $\pm0.5$\,mrad, the injected-orbit stability at the septum, rather than magnet errors in the transfer line, determines whether the window can be used: the assumed booster extraction jitter must be reduced, or the transfer-line optics chosen to reduce its transmission to the septum, before capture comparable to the nominal value can be expected.

The limitations are explicit. The ring is tracked without magnet errors or chamber apertures; the NKM is a thin kick derived from field integrals (a thick-magnet check lowers capture by about 4 percentage points); the kick map is a magnetostatic calculation without measurement; the error budget is assumed rather than derived from component specifications; and capture is defined as survival over 500 turns, not as lifetime. A dedicated optimisation of the injected-beam optics at the septum and of the ring sextupoles, as done at BESSY~II~\cite{Gora2023}, is expected to enlarge the window and was not attempted here.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

For a wire-based NKM in a 4\,GeV ring with a $\DAxMinMm$ to $+\DAxMaxMm$\,mm on-momentum dynamic aperture, nonlinear tracking with a coupled transfer line shows that angle-matched injection near the kick-map extremum captures \RepFieldmapCapture\,\% of the bunch, \RepDipoleCapture\,\% for an equally strong uniform kick, with an angular tolerance of about $\pm0.5$\,mrad. The aligned kicker leaves the stored beam essentially undisturbed; transparency is limited by kicker-to-orbit alignment at the 0.1--0.2\,mm level. Under the assumed combined errors the mean capture is \RobMeanCapture\,\%, limited by the injected-orbit error transmitted from booster extraction. Linear one-turn-map models overestimate capture and should not be used to design such injection systems.
