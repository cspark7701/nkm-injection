# Cover letter (draft for author review — not sent)

Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript "Finite-length and vertical effects in nonlinear-kicker injection into the Korea Light
Source" for publication as a regular paper.

Single nonlinear or multipole injection kickers make top-up injection transparent for the stored beam, but the
injected bunch samples a strongly nonlinear field, and fourth-generation lattices leave little dynamic aperture to
absorb its variation. The manuscript studies a 0.525 m four-loop wire nonlinear kicker magnet (NKM) for the 4 GeV Korea
Light Source (KLS), whose on-momentum dynamic aperture at the kicker is about −12 to +13 mm. The kicker is modelled by
the exact three-dimensional field of its current filaments, validated against the RADIA kick map; the injected beam is
tracked from the booster through the transfer line; and the ring is tracked with native nonlinear 6-D elements, with
paired, calibrated control kickers.

The principal findings are:
- Two effects that kick-map models miss change the injection physics. The beam's own deflection inside the magnet
  shifts the matched angle by 0.2 mrad and makes capture tolerant to one sign of angle error. The curl-free vertical
  field off the kick extremum defocuses the off-axis bunch and costs up to five percentage points of capture.
- At the selected operating point the NKM captures 93.0 % of the injected particles over 500 turns, compared with
  97.0 % for an equally strong uniform kick. The angular window at 80 % capture is 0.74 mrad wide.
- For the aligned kicker the stored-beam disturbance vanishes by symmetry. Transparency is set by the kicker-to-orbit
  offset: 0.10 of the rms beam size at 0.2 mm, confirmed by 1000-turn tracking with radiation.
- Under an assumed combined error budget the mean capture is 44.4 % with ideal static orbit correction of the transfer
  line and 40.5 % without. Reducing the booster jitter to a quarter of its assumed value raises it to 70 %; the
  remaining loss comes from the combined static errors, so a mean of 80 % additionally needs tighter static tolerances
  or a wider window.

The closest previous work comprises the KEK-PF pulsed-sextupole injection, the BESSY II non-linear kicker, the MAX IV and
SOLEIL multipole injection kickers and the SIRIUS NLK; Liu et al. identified kick nonlinearity and injected-beam size as
the efficiency limit. The present article adds the finite-length and vertical mechanisms, the operating window,
transparency tolerances and booster-jitter tolerance curves for a specific design with a small dynamic aperture. The
study is simulation-only and makes no operational claim. All figures and numbers are generated from hashed simulation
artifacts; the code is publicly available, and a frozen evidence bundle is available to referees.

The manuscript has not been submitted elsewhere. Generative AI assistance used in its preparation is declared in the
acknowledgments.
