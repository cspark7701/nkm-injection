\begin{abstract}
A nonlinear kicker magnet (NKM) injects off axis while leaving an aligned stored beam undisturbed, but the injected bunch samples a strongly nonlinear field. We study a 0.525\,m four-loop wire NKM for the 4\,GeV Korea Light Source (KLS), whose on-momentum dynamic aperture at the kicker is about 12\,mm, with an exact three-dimensional filament field, a transfer line tracked from the booster and native nonlinear ring tracking. Two effects that kick-map models miss change the injection physics. The beam's own deflection inside the magnet shifts the matched angle by 0.2\,mrad and makes capture tolerant to one sign of angle error, and the vertical field off the kick extremum defocuses the off-axis bunch, costing up to five percentage points of capture. At the selected operating point the NKM captures \ValThreeD\,\% of the injected particles over 500 turns, compared with \ValDipole\,\% for an equally strong uniform kick. For the aligned kicker the stored-beam disturbance vanishes by symmetry; transparency is set by the kicker-to-orbit offset (\TranspTwoAmp\ of the rms size at 0.2\,mm). Under the assumed combined errors the mean capture is \RobCMean\,\% with ideal static orbit correction of the transfer line (\RobMean\,\% without); reducing the booster jitter to a quarter of its assumed value raises it to \JitCQuarterMean\,\%, and the remaining loss comes from the combined static errors.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Beam dynamics; Accelerator modelling and simulations (multi-particle dynamics, single-particle dynamics); Instrumentation for particle accelerators and storage rings - high energy (linear accelerators, synchrotrons)

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fourth-generation storage rings reduce the electron emittance by an order of magnitude or more with multibend-achromat lattices~\cite{Hettel2014,Raimondi2021}. The strong focusing and sextupoles that achieve this leave a small dynamic aperture (DA), which becomes the main constraint on injection. These rings also run in top-up mode~\cite{Emery1999}: the beam is replenished every few minutes during user operation, so each injection should leave the stored beam undisturbed. Where the DA is too small for off-axis injection, the beam can be injected on axis by swapping bunches with an accumulator~\cite{Emery2003} or injected into the longitudinal acceptance~\cite{Aiba2015}. Where off-axis injection remains possible, the conventional four-kicker orbit bump leaves residual oscillations because the kicker pulses cannot be matched perfectly.

A single pulsed multipole whose field vanishes on the stored-beam axis avoids this. Injection with a pulsed quadrupole was demonstrated at the KEK PF-AR~\cite{Harada2007} and with a pulsed sextupole at the KEK Photon Factory~\cite{Takaki2010}. A non-linear kicker (NLK) with an off-axis field maximum was developed at BESSY~II~\cite{Atkinson2011,Gora2023}, and a multipole injection kicker (MIK) met a transparency specification of 10\,\% of the rms beam size at MAX~IV and reached 2\,\% at SOLEIL~\cite{Alexandre2021,Kallestrup2019,Ollier2023}; because commissioning pulsed-multipole injection is not straightforward, a single dipole kicker was added at MAX~IV for the commissioning stage~\cite{Leemann2012}. At SIRIUS, NLK injection was predicted to reach 99\,\% efficiency for a $-9$\,mm DA but delivered about $85\pm8$\,\% with the design sextupoles, and online sextupole optimisation was needed~\cite{Liu2016,Velloso2023}; BESSY~II likewise reached up to 97\,\% only with optimised sextupoles~\cite{Gora2023}.

The price of transparency is a kick that varies across the injected bunch. Liu \emph{et al.}~\cite{Liu2016} identified this nonlinearity and the injected-beam size as the main efficiency limit, and placing the bunch near the kick extremum is the natural remedy. Design studies usually describe the kicker by a kick map, the straight-line field integral applied at the magnet centre~\cite{Elleaume1992}. In a strongly nonlinear kicker this approximation is not controlled: the injected beam is deflected by several milliradians inside the magnet, and the vertical kick off the midplane follows from the field's curl-free structure, which a tabulated map does not enforce. Integrating the field along the actual path, as is done for insertion devices and fringe fields~\cite{Wu2003}, removes both approximations.

The Korea Light Source (KLS) is a 4\,GeV, 28-cell hybrid multibend-achromat ring of about 800\,m circumference with a natural emittance of 62\,pm~\cite{Kim2025}. This article studies these effects for a 0.525\,m four-loop wire NKM in a KLS design lattice, whose on-momentum horizontal DA at the injection point extends from $\DAxMinMm$ to $+\DAxMaxMm$\,mm. The kicker is modelled by the exact three-dimensional field of its current filaments, validated against the RADIA~\cite{Chubar1998} kick map, and tracked through in slices. The contributions are: (i) the identification of the beam's self-deflection inside the magnet as the finite-length mechanism, and a magnet-length trade-off; (ii) the vertical defocusing of the off-axis bunch, previously hidden by a sign error in the tabulated vertical kick; (iii) transparency as an exact symmetry, with alignment and residual-field tolerances; and (iv) capture tolerance curves against booster jitter, kicker offset and injected emittance. The study is simulation-only and uses an ideal lattice with bounded sensitivities to ring errors and chamber apertures; it makes no operational claim.

It is a design version, not the latest KLS lattice; its file identifier is retained because an independent release date was not supplied. \subsection{Kicker field and tracking}

The 0.525\,m four-loop magnet is a preliminary design for this study by Dong-Eon Kim (Pohang Accelerator Laboratory), as stated by the author; it is not the KLS baseline injection kicker. The baseline uses a four-kicker orbit bump and a 1\,mm thin septum~\cite{Hahn2024}. The alternative NKM scheme studied here retains an assumed 2\,mm blade and a stored-beam edge at $-16$\,mm; these are study geometry, not the published baseline septum. Loop powering has not been specified, so series and independent-loop cases are distinguished in the transparency tolerances.

The NKM consists of four closed rectangular filament loops, one per quadrant, each with an inner and an outer longitudinal wire joined by end connectors (figure~\ref{fig:geom}, left). The field of straight filaments in vacuum is evaluated in closed form (Biot--Savart), including the fringe and end-connector fields. Its straight-line integrals reproduce both components of the RADIA kick map to \MapAgreeRad\,rad (\MapAgreeRel\ relative) on \MapNodes\ nodes, so the RADIA calculation and the filament model describe the same field. The tabulated map stores $(\int B_y\,\mathrm{d}s, \int B_x\,\mathrm{d}s)/B\rho$; the curl-free kick pair, $\partial\Delta x'/\partial y=\partial\Delta y'/\partial x$, requires $-\int B_x\,\mathrm{d}s$ for the vertical kick. Using the map's vertical sign therefore turns vertical defocusing into focusing; all thin-kick results below use the corrected sign.

The magnet is tracked in 640 drift--kick--drift slices over $\pm0.45$\,m in canonical AT coordinates, $\mathrm{d}p_x/\mathrm{d}s = PS(B_y-y'B_s)/B\rho$ and $\mathrm{d}p_y/\mathrm{d}s=PS(-B_x+x'B_s)/B\rho$ with slopes $x'=p_x/(1+\delta)$, polarity $P$ and field scale $S$. The kick acts on canonical momentum and carries no $1/(1+\delta)$ factor; the exit angle changes by \DeltaSensUrad\,$\mu$rad per $10^{-3}$ in $\delta$. Slice and span refinement change the exit angle by less than \SliceConvRad\,rad. Particles within 1\,mm of a conductor are counted as lost; none was. The polarity is chosen so that the kick at $x<0$ cancels the angle of a beam travelling from the septum towards the axis (figure~\ref{fig:geom}, right).

\subsection{Injected beam, capture and controls}

The injected bunch is generated at booster extraction and tracked element by element through the matched BTS line to the septum exit, where it is placed at $(x_\mathrm{inj}, x'_\mathrm{inj})$. It drifts to the magnet, is tracked through the 3-D NKM once (single-turn pulse, section~\ref{sec:pulse}) and then for 500 turns. \emph{Capture} is the fraction of injected particles alive after the last turn; intervals are 95\,\% Wilson intervals~\cite{Wilson1927} for particles and cluster-bootstrap intervals for error realizations. Lost particles are classified as lost at septum creation, inside the magnet, at the septum on a later turn, or elsewhere in the ring; the categories reconcile with the particle count. The \emph{matched} angle is the inner root of zero centroid angle after the magnet. Two controls receive identical particles: a uniform \emph{dipole} kick and a \emph{linearised} kick (with its curl-free vertical partner), both calibrated to the 3-D response of the nominal centroid.

\section{Finite-length and vertical effects}
\label{sec:mechanism}

At the operating point of section~\ref{sec:window} the thin kick map gives a matched angle of \MatchThinMrad\,mrad, the 3-D magnet \MatchThreeDMrad\,mrad. To isolate the cause, the map's horizontal profile was distributed over the magnet length and sampled either along the undeflected straight line or along the self-consistent deflected path (figure~\ref{fig:mech}). Straight-path sampling gives \MatchStraightMrad\,mrad; the deflected path gives \MatchBentMrad\,mrad, equal to the 3-D result, and reproduces its response to angle errors to 0.01\,mm. The finite-length effect is therefore the beam's own deflection inside the magnet, with a sagitta $|K|L/8=\SagittaMm$\,mm, not path averaging or field details.

The effect is asymmetric. A positive septum angle error moves the trajectory inwards; on the straight line it would cross the kick extremum onto the weaker inner flank, leaving the extra angle under-compensated. The self-deflection instead holds the trajectory on the outer side of the extremum, where the kick is largest (figure~\ref{fig:mech}, centre). A $+0.3$\,mrad error changes the betatron amplitude of the kicked centroid by \AmpThinPlus\,mm with the thin kick but by \AmpThreeDPlus\,mm with the 3-D magnet, whereas $-0.3$\,mrad gives \AmpThinMinus\ and \AmpThreeDMinus\,mm; the nominal amplitude is larger (\AmpThreeDNom\ against \AmpThinNom\,mm). At fixed integrated kick, a longer magnet lowers the sensitivity to positive errors but raises the nominal amplitude, and for $L\geq\LengthNoMatchM$\,m no matched solution remains (figure~\ref{fig:mech}, right). A kick map is adequate only when the sagitta is small compared with the width of the kick extremum.

The vertical field matters as much. Near the kick extremum the 3-D magnet defocuses vertically: a centroid at $y=1$\,mm receives \VertKickThreeD\,mrad, against \VertKickThin\,mrad from the sign-corrected thin map, because the deflected path samples the field off the straight line. On identical bunches at the earlier operating point, capture is \CapThinLegacy\,\% with the map's original vertical sign, \CapThinPhys\,\% with the corrected sign and \CapThreeDRematch\,\% with the 3-D magnet. Suppressing only the 3-D vertical kick restores \CapThreeDNoVert\,\% (the horizontal-only finite-length model gives \CapThickOneD\,\%), so at that point the vertical defocusing costs about five percentage points. The linear vertical defocusing equals $-\partial\Delta x'/\partial x$ and vanishes at the kick extremum; at the operating point selected in section~\ref{sec:window}, where the centroid sits at the extremum, the cost is about two points (section~\ref{sec:survival}).

\section{Survival, acceptance and sensitivity}
\label{sec:survival}

Tracking \SurvN\ particles for \SurvLong\ turns, about one damping time, \SurvShort\,\% survive 500 turns, \SurvMid\,\% survive 2000 turns and \SurvLong\,\% the full run; \SurvLateLossPct\,\% of the particles are lost after turn 500 (figure~\ref{fig:accept}, right). A single \BigN-particle bunch gives \BigCap\,\% (\BigLo--\BigHi\,\%): \BigLossSeptumReturn\ particles are lost at the septum on later turns, \BigLossElsewhere\ elsewhere in the ring, \BigLossCreation\ at creation and \BigLossMagnet\ inside the magnet. With a uniform rectangular chamber of half-height 5\,mm and half-width 15, 12, 11 or 10\,mm at every element, capture is \AperFifteenCap, \AperTwelveCap, \AperElevenCap\ and \AperTenCap\,\%, respectively. The loss at 15\,mm is vertical: a 10\,mm half-height restores \AperDiagYten\,\%, and an open horizontal aperture with a 5\,mm half-height gives \AperDiagXopen\,\%. On the same particles the 5\,mm half-height costs the same with and without the NKM vertical kick (\AperDiagRef\ to \AperDiagXopen\,\% and \AperDiagOpenNoVert\ to \AperDiagNoVert\,\%), so these losses come from the ring's coupling of the large horizontal oscillation into the vertical plane, not from the kicker. At the selected point the NKM vertical kick itself costs only the difference between \AperDiagOpenNoVert\ and \AperDiagRef\,\%, less than at the earlier point of section~\ref{sec:mechanism}, because the centroid now sits at the kick extremum, where the linear vertical defocusing $-\partial\Delta x'/\partial x$ vanishes. The vertical stay-clear at large horizontal amplitude is therefore a design constraint of the injection straight. Random relative gradient errors of \RingErrLowRel\,\% and \RingErrHighRel\,\% on all quadrupoles and \RingErrSextRel\,\% on all sextupoles (\RingErrLowSeeds\ seeds each, no misalignments, no correction) reduce the negative DA edge to between \RingErrLowDAmin\ and \RingErrLowDAmax\,mm and between \RingErrHighDAmin\ and \RingErrHighDAmax\,mm, and give mean capture \RingErrLowMean\,\% (range \RingErrLowMin--\RingErrLowMax\,\%) and \RingErrHighMean\,\% (\RingErrHighMin--\RingErrHighMax\,\%). Because these errors are uncorrected, they bound the sensitivity rather than predict a corrected machine; the injection DA of the corrected KLS lattice is the decisive input for an operational estimate.

\section{Stored-beam transparency}
\label{sec:transparency}

The kick of the four-loop magnet is odd in $x$ on the midplane and its near-axis expansion is purely cubic, $\Delta x'\simeq c_3x^3$ with $c_3={}$\CubicCoeff\,rad\,m$^{-3}$ at $S=\OpScale$. For an aligned kicker and a centred Gaussian beam the centroid kick therefore vanishes exactly; the value $5\times10^{-6}$ of the rms size quoted from a $10^5$-particle sample in earlier work equals its sampling standard error (\TranspMCsem). The meaningful quantities are the response to an offset between kicker and stored orbit and the emittance change, evaluated deterministically from the exact field by Gauss--Hermite quadrature (figure~\ref{fig:transp}, right). Offsets of 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5\,mm give centroid amplitudes $A_x$ of \TranspOneAmp, \TranspTwoAmp\ and \TranspFiveAmp\ of the rms size, and the spread of the kick across the beam increases the emittance about the displaced centroid by \TranspOneEmit, \TranspTwoEmit\ and \TranspFiveEmit; a 0.2\,mm vertical offset gives \TranspVertTwoAmp\ of the vertical rms size. Once the centroid oscillation has also filamented, the rms emittance grows by the factor $1+A_x^2/2+\Delta\varepsilon_x/\varepsilon_x$, i.e.\ \TranspTwoTotal\ at 0.2\,mm and \TranspFiveTotal\ at 0.5\,mm. Tracking the stored beam through the 3-D magnet and the ring for \TrackedTurns\ turns (radiation on) reproduces both estimates: $A_x/\sigma_x=\TrackedTwoAmp$ and \TrackedFiveAmp, and emittance ratios \TrackedTwoEmitRatio\ and \TrackedFiveEmitRatio\ at the last turn. An offset of 0.2\,mm thus keeps the stored-beam disturbance at about a tenth of its size, whereas 0.5\,mm is not transparent. The bicubic interpolation of the map agrees with the exact field to \BicubicErr\,rad near the axis, whereas bilinear interpolation errs by \BilinearErr\,rad and overstates the 0.1\,mm response nineteen-fold (\TranspBilinOne). The calibrated dipole control would displace the stored beam by about \CtrlDipoleAmp\ rms sizes in a linear estimate; this hypothetical figure only illustrates why a uniform kicker cannot be used for top-up.

\section{Robustness and tolerances}
\label{sec:robust}

\subsection{Error budget}

Table~\ref{tab:errors} lists the assumed errors. They are design hypotheses, not component specifications; static errors are drawn once per realization, the booster centroid, energy and NKM amplitude and timing errors represent shot-to-shot jitter. Timing jitter is represented as a field-scale change that gives the same kick change for the injected centroid; unlike an additive dipole kick it leaves the field zero on axis. With the matched BTS optics fixed, \TolN\ realizations give a median horizontal exit mismatch $M_x=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_0^{-1}\Sigma)-1$ (zero for matched Twiss parameters; $\Sigma$, $\Sigma_0$ the normalised beam matrices at the BTS exit and its target) of $\TolMedianMx\times10^{-3}$ (95\,\% interval \TolMedianMxLo--\TolMedianMxHi$\times10^{-3}$), and \TolFailures\ exceed the 60\,m $\beta$ limit; the optics errors are therefore minor for capture.

\subsection{Combined errors and tolerance curves}

Two ensembles bound the treatment of static transfer-line errors. Without orbit correction, the static trajectory errors of the transfer line reach the septum; with ideal steering at the septum exit, the static trajectory of each perturbed line is removed and only the shot-to-shot errors remain. Over \RobN\ realizations from three seeds (200 particles, 500 turns), the 3-D NKM captures \RobMean\,\% on average without correction (\RobMeanLo--\RobMeanHi\,\%, median \RobMedian\,\%) and \RobCMean\,\% with correction (\RobCMeanLo--\RobCMeanHi\,\%, median \RobCMedian\,\%), against \RobDipMean\ and \RobCDipMean\,\% for the dipole control on the same realizations (paired differences \RobNkmMinusDip\ and \RobCNkmMinusDip\,points, 95\,\% intervals \RobNkmMinusDipLo\ to \RobNkmMinusDipHi\ and \RobCNkmMinusDipLo\ to \RobCNkmMinusDipHi); the realization without errors captures \RobZeroErr\,\% (dipole \RobZeroErrDip\,\%) (figure~\ref{fig:robust}, left). The deficit with respect to the dipole thus grows from about two points without errors to about twenty with them: the NKM kick falls off on both sides of its extremum, so its angular window (section~\ref{sec:window}) is narrower than that of the uniform kick, and realizations whose transfer-line exit angle deviates by more than 0.2\,mrad lose most. Controlling for all other sampled errors (partial rank correlation), the booster angle jitter is the dominant driver for both kickers ($\rho=-0.45$ for the NKM and $-0.68$ for the dipole without correction, $-0.52$ and $-0.75$ with it); once the static offset is corrected, the booster position jitter also becomes significant ($\rho=-0.24$ for both).

Scaling only the booster centroid jitter on paired realizations (figure~\ref{fig:robust}, centre) separates jitter from static errors. With correction, the mean capture is \JitCFullMean\,\% at the assumed jitter, \JitCHalfMean\,\% at half, \JitCQuarterMean\,\% at a quarter and \JitCZeroMean\,\% without jitter; below a quarter of the assumed amplitude (0.125\,mm, 0.05\,mrad) the jitter costs nothing measurable. Position jitter alone gives \JitCXOnlyMean\,\% and angle jitter alone \JitCXpOnlyMean\,\%. Without correction, removing the jitter raises the mean only to \JitZeroMean\,\%, because the static transfer-line errors displace the exit centroid by 0.7\,mm rms, more than the position window. Even with correction and without jitter the mean stays below 80\,\% (median \JitCZeroMedian\,\%, \JitCZeroFracEighty\,\% of realizations above 80\,\%): the remaining loss comes from the combined static errors of table~\ref{tab:errors} (kicker, orbit and septum offsets, energy and dipole-field errors), none of which dominates. A mean capture of 80\,\% under this budget therefore needs, besides orbit correction and booster jitter of at most a quarter of the assumed value, either tighter static tolerances or a wider window, for example from sextupole optimisation for the injection DA. Without other errors, capture varies between \OffCapMin\ and \OffCapMax\,\% for NKM-to-orbit offsets within $\pm\OffRangeMm$\,mm, and from \EmitTwentyFiveCap\,\% at an injected emittance of 25\,nm\,rad to \EmitTwoHundredCap\,\% at 200\,nm\,rad.

\section{Pulsed-magnet considerations}
\label{sec:pulse}

The results above apply to an ideal single-pass spatial field. The revolution period is \RevPeriodUs\,$\mu$s. On the turn after injection the injected centroid passes the NKM at \TurnOneXmm\,mm, where the full field would kick by \TurnOneKickMrad\,mrad; on the second and third turns it passes at \TurnTwoXmm\ and \TurnThreeXmm\,mm with full-field kicks of \TurnTwoKickMrad\ and \TurnThreeKickMrad\,mrad. If a fraction $r$ of the field remains on later turns, the residual kick should stay below one tenth of the angular window width (\ResidualAllowMrad\,mrad), which requires $r\lesssim\ResidualTurnOne$ on the first and $r\lesssim\ResidualTurnTwo$ on the second following turn. For the stored beam the residual and pulse shape scale the response of section~\ref{sec:transparency} and vanish for an aligned kicker; any on-axis dipole residual, for example from chamber eddy currents or asymmetries, must stay below \DipoleResidualRel\ of the peak kick to keep the centroid amplitude below 10\,\% of the rms size, and a gradient residual below \GradResidualRel\ of the peak gradient to keep the tune change below $10^{-4}$. For a half-sine pulse of length 5--8\,$\mu$s, a timing jitter of 1--5\,ns changes the kick by $10^{-7}$--$10^{-6}$ relative. Coating-induced fields, observed as quadrupole components in an MIK~\cite{Kallestrup2019}, are outside this study.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Four results generalise beyond this design. First, the kick map is not a controlled description of a strongly nonlinear injection kicker: the self-deflection inside the magnet, with sagitta $|K|L/8$, shifts the matched angle and changes the error sensitivity in a sign-dependent way, and the magnet length is a design variable that trades nominal amplitude against tolerance. Second, the curl-free vertical kick defocuses the injected bunch wherever the horizontal kick gradient is non-zero and costs up to several points of capture, which favours placing the centroid at the extremum; tabulated maps should be checked for $\partial\Delta x'/\partial y=\partial\Delta y'/\partial x$. Third, the transparency of an aligned NKM is a symmetry property; the engineering quantities are the kicker-to-orbit alignment, the on-axis residual field and the pulse length. Fourth, with an angular window of order \WinAngleWidth\,mrad and a position window of about \WinPosWidth\,mm, the injected orbit at the septum, not the kick strength or the transfer-line optics, decides whether the window can be used: static transfer-line errors must be corrected, the booster jitter kept to a quarter of the assumed amplitude, and the remaining static offsets of kicker, orbit and septum budgeted together.

The limitations are explicit. The ring is an ideal lattice; ring errors and apertures enter only as bounded sensitivities. The filament model contains no iron, chamber or eddy-current fields and no pulse time dependence. The error budget is assumed. Capture is survival over 500 turns, checked to one damping time at the selected point. As at BESSY~II and SIRIUS~\cite{Gora2023,Velloso2023}, sextupole optimisation for the injection DA is expected to enlarge the window and was not attempted.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

For a 0.525\,m wire NKM in the 4\,GeV KLS design lattice, exact 3-D field tracking with a coupled transfer line gives \ValThreeD\,\% capture at the selected point (\ValDipole\,\% for an equally strong uniform kick), an angular window of \WinAngleWidth\,mrad at \WinThreshold\,\% capture, and a stored-beam disturbance that vanishes for the aligned kicker and is \TranspTwoAmp\ of the rms size at a 0.2\,mm offset. Kick-map models miss the beam's self-deflection in the magnet and, with the tabulated vertical sign, the vertical defocusing of the injected bunch; both change capture by several points. Under the assumed errors the mean capture is \RobCMean\,\% with ideal static orbit correction and \RobMean\,\% without; jitter below a quarter of the assumed amplitude costs nothing, and a mean capture of 80\,\% additionally requires tighter static tolerances or a wider window.
