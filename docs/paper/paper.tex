\title{Finite-length and vertical effects in nonlinear-kicker injection into a Korea Light Source design lattice}

\author{Chong Shik Park}
\email{kuphy@korea.ac.kr}
\affiliation{Department of Accelerator Science and Center for Accelerator Research,
Korea University, Sejong 30019, Republic of Korea}
\date{October 12, 2026}



\begin{abstract}
Segmented tracking through the conductor field of a nonlinear injection kicker is established, but the accuracy of a single thin kick map has not been quantified. For a 0.525\,m four-loop magnet in a 4\,GeV Korea Light Source design lattice, we compare tracking in an exact filament field, thin and sliced vector maps, and an independent adaptive trajectory solver. A sagitta-to-kick-width ratio organizes finite-length differences over two related wire families; vertical focusing depends on the sampled flank. With the design booster beam and a declared chamber, nominal capture is \ChamberNom\,\% at 500 turns and \FinalLong\,\% at 4000 turns. Under the assumed joint machine/shot budget, mean capture is \FinalNkmMean\,\%, conditional on ten corrected rings; these values are not operational efficiency predictions. Ideal centred centroid cancellation also does not ensure full-beam transparency: finite moments change, and wire placement and current imbalance impose separate tolerances. Conditional jitter differences and held-out capture-based tuning quantify the remaining margin without asserting a universal injection tolerance.


\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fourth-generation storage rings reduce the electron emittance by an order of magnitude or more with multibend-achromat lattices~\cite{Hettel2014,Raimondi2021}. The strong focusing and sextupoles that achieve this leave a small dynamic aperture (DA), which becomes the main constraint on injection. These rings also run in top-up mode~\cite{Emery1999}: the beam is replenished every few minutes during user operation, so each injection should leave the stored beam undisturbed. Where the DA is too small for off-axis injection, the beam can be injected on axis by swapping bunches with an accumulator~\cite{Emery2003} or injected into the longitudinal acceptance~\cite{Aiba2015}. Where off-axis injection remains possible, the conventional four-kicker orbit bump leaves residual oscillations because the kicker pulses cannot be matched perfectly.

A single pulsed multipole whose field vanishes on the stored-beam axis avoids this. Injection with a pulsed quadrupole was demonstrated at the KEK PF-AR~\cite{Harada2007} and with a pulsed sextupole at the KEK Photon Factory~\cite{Takaki2010}. A non-linear kicker (NLK) with an off-axis field maximum was developed at BESSY~II~\cite{Atkinson2011,Gora2023}, and a multipole injection kicker (MIK) met a transparency specification of 10\,\% of the rms beam size at MAX~IV and reached 2\,\% at SOLEIL~\cite{Alexandre2021,Kallestrup2019,Ollier2023}; because commissioning pulsed-multipole injection is not straightforward, a single dipole kicker was added at MAX~IV for the commissioning stage~\cite{Leemann2012}. At SIRIUS, NLK injection was predicted to reach 99\,\% efficiency for a $-9$\,mm DA but delivered about $85\pm8$\,\% with the design sextupoles, and online sextupole optimisation was needed~\cite{Liu2016,Velloso2023}; BESSY~II likewise reached up to 97\,\% only with optimised sextupoles~\cite{Gora2023}.

The price of transparency is a kick that varies across the injected bunch. Liu \emph{et al.}~\cite{Liu2016} identified this nonlinearity and the injected-beam size as the main efficiency limit, and placing the bunch near the kick extremum is the natural remedy. Design studies usually describe the kicker by a kick map, the straight-line field integral applied at the magnet centre~\cite{Elleaume1992}. A single thin map requires a finite-length accuracy check: the injected beam is deflected by several milliradians inside the magnet, and the vertical kick off the midplane follows from the field's curl-free structure, which requires a consistently signed vector map. Integrating the field along the actual path, as is done for insertion devices and fringe fields~\cite{Wu2003}, resolves trajectory displacement while preserving the physical vector field. For nonlinear kickers this is established practice: Sun \emph{et al.}~\cite{Sun2020} optimized an eight-conductor ALS kicker with segmented drift--kick--drift tracking through a Biot--Savart field, checked against Runge--Kutta integration and a finite three-dimensional conductor model with return loops, and found that the finite-conductor fringe field did not significantly affect injection efficiency. That kicker is 0.28\,m long, receives the beam almost parallel (0.08\,mrad at the septum) and kicks by about 1\,mrad, as estimated from the published field profile; its trajectory sagitta inside the magnet is therefore of order 0.04\,mm.

The Korea Light Source (KLS) is a 4\,GeV, 28-cell hybrid multibend-achromat ring of about 800\,m circumference with a natural emittance of 62\,pm~\cite{Kim2025}. This article studies these effects for a 0.525\,m four-loop wire NKM in a KLS design lattice, with the chamber-limited acceptance reported below. The kicker is modelled by the exact three-dimensional field of its current filaments, validated against the RADIA~\cite{Chubar1998} kick map, and tracked through in slices. The tracking tools are therefore not new. The present contribution is a quantified approximation-error result for the opposite regime: the kicker cancels a 4.85\,mrad incoming angle within 0.525\,m, so the sagitta (0.32\,mm) is a few percent of the kick-lobe width. In this regime a single thin map shifts the matched angle and changes the sign-dependent error response, while a sliced vector map does not. The sagitta-to-lobe-width ratio organizes the thin-map error over two wire families; the ALS sagitta is about an order of magnitude smaller than here, consistent with its published finding. An independent off-midplane trajectory check and conditional capture and transparency tolerances complete the study. The signed vertical field is a consistency requirement for vector maps, not a new deficiency of kick-map methods. The study includes a declared chamber and ten corrected-error rings; it does not predict commissioned-machine performance.

\subsection{Kicker field and tracking}

The 0.525\,m four-loop magnet is a preliminary design for this study by Dong-Eon Kim (Pohang Accelerator Laboratory), as stated by the author; it is not the KLS baseline injection kicker. The baseline uses a four-kicker orbit bump and a 1\,mm thin septum~\cite{Hahn2024}. The alternative NKM scheme studied here retains an assumed 2\,mm blade and a stored-beam edge at $-16$\,mm; these are study geometry, not the published baseline septum. Loop powering has not been specified, so series and independent-loop cases are distinguished in the transparency tolerances.

The NKM consists of four closed rectangular filament loops, one per quadrant, each with an inner and an outer longitudinal wire joined by end connectors (figure~\ref{fig:geom}, left). The field of straight filaments in vacuum is evaluated in closed form (Biot--Savart), including the fringe and end-connector fields. Its straight-line integrals reproduce both components of the RADIA kick map to \MapAgreeRad\,rad (\MapAgreeRel\ relative) on \MapNodes\ nodes, so the RADIA calculation and the filament model describe the same field. The tabulated map stores $(\int B_y\,\mathrm{d}s, \int B_x\,\mathrm{d}s)/B\rho$; the curl-free kick pair, $\partial\Delta x'/\partial y=\partial\Delta y'/\partial x$, requires $-\int B_x\,\mathrm{d}s$ for the vertical kick. Interpreting the tabulated field component as the vertical kick without the coordinate sign reverses the vertical response; physical thin-map comparisons use the coordinate-consistent sign; the reversed-sign diagnostic is labelled explicitly.

The magnet is tracked in 640 drift--kick--drift slices over $\pm0.45$\,m in canonical AT coordinates, $\mathrm{d}p_x/\mathrm{d}s = PS(B_y-y'B_s)/B\rho$ and $\mathrm{d}p_y/\mathrm{d}s=PS(-B_x+x'B_s)/B\rho$ with slopes $x'=p_x/(1+\delta)$, polarity $P$ and field scale $S$. The kick acts on canonical momentum and carries no $1/(1+\delta)$ factor; the exit angle changes by \DeltaSensUrad\,$\mu$rad per $10^{-3}$ in $\delta$. Slice and span refinement change the exit angle by less than \SliceConvRad\,rad. Particles within 1\,mm of a conductor are counted as lost. The polarity is chosen so that the kick at $x<0$ cancels the angle of a beam travelling from the septum towards the axis (figure~\ref{fig:geom}, right).

\subsection{Injected beam, capture and controls}

The injected bunch is generated at booster extraction and tracked element by element through the matched BTS line to the septum exit, where it is placed at $(x_\mathrm{inj}, x'_\mathrm{inj})$. It propagates to the magnet, is tracked through the 3-D NKM once (single-turn pulse, section~\ref{sec:pulse}) and then for 500 turns. \emph{Capture} is the fraction of injected particles alive after the last turn; intervals are 95\,\% Wilson intervals~\cite{Wilson1927} for particles and cluster-bootstrap intervals for error realizations. Lost particles are classified by upstream transport, septum creation, incoming transport, magnet domain, return septum or elsewhere in the ring; the categories reconcile with the particle count. The \emph{matched} angle is the inner root of zero centroid angle after the magnet. Two controls receive identical particles: a uniform \emph{dipole} kick and a \emph{linearised} kick (with its curl-free vertical partner), both calibrated to the 3-D response of the nominal centroid. This angular calibration does not reproduce every finite-length position--angle correlation, so it cannot uniquely decompose the capture difference into a nonlinear-kick contribution.

\section{Finite-length and vertical effects}
\label{sec:mechanism}

At a diagnostic launch $x_\mathrm{inj}=-20$\,mm and $S=0.85$, the thin kick map gives a matched angle of \MatchThinMrad\,mrad, the 3-D magnet \MatchThreeDMrad\,mrad. To isolate the cause, the map's horizontal profile was distributed over the magnet length and sampled either along the undeflected straight line or along the self-consistent deflected path (figure~\ref{fig:mech}). Straight-path sampling gives \MatchStraightMrad\,mrad; the deflected path gives \MatchBentMrad\,mrad, equal to the 3-D result, and reproduces its response to angle errors to 0.01\,mm. Straight-path sampling already shifts the thin-map match, and self-deflection produces an additional shift. Its sagitta is $|K|L/8=\SagittaMm$\,mm. Agreement between the deflected-profile and 3-D calculations supports this mechanism in the tested configuration; it does not make longitudinal sampling or end fields universally irrelevant.

The effect is asymmetric. A positive septum angle error moves the trajectory inwards; on the straight line it would cross the kick extremum onto the weaker inner flank, leaving the extra angle under-compensated. The self-deflection instead holds the trajectory on the outer side of the extremum, where the kick is largest (figure~\ref{fig:mech}, centre). A $+0.3$\,mrad error changes the betatron amplitude of the kicked centroid by \AmpThinPlus\,mm with the thin kick but by \AmpThreeDPlus\,mm with the 3-D magnet, whereas $-0.3$\,mrad gives \AmpThinMinus\ and \AmpThreeDMinus\,mm; the nominal amplitude is larger (\AmpThreeDNom\ against \AmpThinNom\,mm). At fixed integrated kick, a longer magnet lowers the sensitivity to positive errors but raises the nominal amplitude, and for $L\geq\LengthNoMatchM$\,m no matched solution remains (figure~\ref{fig:mech}, right). Small sagitta relative to the kick-lobe width supports a thin-map approximation; a sliced vector map can resolve the deflection when that ratio is appreciable.

At this diagnostic launch the 3-D magnet defocuses vertically: a centroid at $y=1$\,mm receives \VertKickThreeD\,mrad, against \VertKickThin\,mrad from the sign-corrected thin map, because the deflected path samples the field off the straight line. For paired 100\,nm\,rad horizontal-emittance sensitivity bunches at this diagnostic launch, capture is \CapThinLegacy\,\% with a reversed vertical-kick sign, \CapThinPhys\,\% with the corrected sign and \CapThreeDRematch\,\% with the 3-D magnet. Suppressing only the 3-D vertical kick restores \CapThreeDNoVert\,\% (the horizontal-only finite-length model gives \CapThickOneD\,\%), so at that point the vertical defocusing costs about five percentage points. For a curl-free thin vector kick, the linear vertical coefficient is $-\partial\Delta x'/\partial x$, with flank-dependent focusing sign, and vanishes at the kick extremum; at the operating point selected in section~\ref{sec:window}, where the centroid sits near the extremum, the approximately two-point cost is a 100\,nm\,rad sensitivity result (section~\ref{sec:survival}), not the design-beam headline.

The sagitta ratio $\eta=|K|L/(8w)$ compares trajectory displacement with the kick structure. Here $K$ is the peak straight-line kick of the magnet, $L$ its length, and $w$ the full width at half maximum of the negative horizontal kick lobe (\GenNomW\,mm for the nominal wires). A trajectory deflected uniformly by $K$ over $L$ is a circular arc whose sagitta relative to its chord is $|K|L/8$; the chord through the kick extremum is the straight-line reference path of a thin map. Figure~\ref{fig:generality} varies $L$ (0.2--1.0\,m), the integrated strength (scales 0.5, 1.0, 1.5) and the transverse wire scale (1.0 and 1.25), giving \GenConfigs\ configurations with $\eta=\GenEtaMin$--\GenEtaMax. Each centroid starts at the straight-line kick extremum with horizontal momentum opposite the kick, $y=1$\,mm and $y'=0.2$\,mrad (both scaled with the wire family), and is tracked by a single thin vector map, a 1280-slice vector map distributed over the wire length, and the exact 1280-slice filament field. Normalizing the exit-momentum error by $|K|$ removes the trivial dependence on kick strength. The thin-map horizontal error then follows $|\Delta p_x|/|K|\simeq\GenPrefX\,\eta^{\GenExpX}$ over all configurations, with a log-rms scatter of \GenScatterX\,\% (largest deviation \GenScatterMaxX\,\%); the two wire families give exponents \GenExpFamilyOne\ and \GenExpFamilyTwo. The vertical error organizes less tightly ($\propto\eta^{\GenExpY}$, scatter \GenScatterY\,\%), because it also depends on the vertical launch. The sliced vector map stays below \GenSlicedMax\ of $|K|$ in both planes. To relate this to injection, we declare an accuracy allowance of one tenth of the 80\,\% angular-window width (\GenAllowMrad\,mrad), the same convention as the pulse allowance of section~\ref{sec:pulse}. At the nominal kick the fitted thin-map error reaches it at $\eta^*\simeq\GenEtaStar$; the nominal magnet ($\eta=\GenNomEta$) uses \GenAllowFraction\,\% of it per pass ($|\Delta p_x|=\GenNomThinDpx$\,mrad, and $|\Delta p_y|=\GenNomThinDpy$\,mrad at the stated vertical launch). These are deterministic comparisons for the tested family and launch recipe, not a universal capture criterion. A sliced vector map retains self-deflection and the physical vertical kick; the local focusing sign changes between flanks of the kick extremum. A reversed vertical-kick sign is an input-convention error, not a failure inherent to kick maps. Capture controls throughout are paired model comparisons on identical particles, not independently optimized injection systems.

\section{Operating window}
\label{sec:window}

At the final point and reference chamber, the native ring captures \FinalBackendNative\,\% at 500 turns, versus \FinalBackendLinear\,\% for an affine element-map reference on identical particles. The reference retains element lengths and explicit aperture checks at physical boundaries, and linearizes about the six-dimensional closed orbit with RF and radiation. A small-perturbation one-turn check agrees within $\FinalBackendTangentMax$ in the corresponding metre/radian/dimensionless coordinates. It deliberately omits nonlinear dynamics; all capture conclusions use native tracking.

Nominal native survival at 4000 turns is \FinalLong\,\% (95\,\% particle Wilson interval \FinalLongLo--\FinalLongHi\,\%), a loss of \FinalLateLoss\ percentage points after turn 500. The 500-turn prefix reproduces the paired chamber-study particle masks exactly. Fresh bunches at the interpolated lower and upper angular-edge candidates give \FinalLowerShort/\FinalUpperShort\,\% at 500 turns and \FinalLowerLong/\FinalUpperLong\,\% at 4000 turns (95\,\% intervals \FinalLowerLongLo--\FinalLowerLongHi\,\% and \FinalUpperLongLo--\FinalUpperLongHi\,\%). The degraded corrected ring 5501 gives \FinalDegradedShort\,\% at 500 turns and \FinalDegradedLong\,\% at 4000 turns (\FinalDegradedLongLo--\FinalDegradedLongHi\,\%). Thus the stated window remains a 500-turn conditional contour, not an infinite-time acceptance or a guaranteed 4000-turn 80\,\% contour.

The point $x_\mathrm{inj}=\OpXinjMm$\,mm, $S=\OpScale$, $x'_\mathrm{inj}=\OpXpMrad$\,mrad was selected by the largest particle-Wilson lower bound in a fixed matched-point grid using a 100/10\,nm\,rad sensitivity beam. Four fresh bunches gave \ValThreeD\,\% (\ValThreeDLo--\ValThreeDHi\,\%) for that beam and septum-only aperture. This independent sample reduces reuse of selection noise; it does not remove dependence on the searched grid or model. The design-beam/chamber studies retain these knobs without reselection and therefore test a conditional operating point rather than a new global optimum.

We estimate the connected operating window above 80\,\% capture around that fixed point. Linear interpolation gives angle offsets from \FinalAngleLo\ to \FinalAngleHi\,mrad (width \FinalAngleWidth\,mrad), and position offsets span \FinalPositionLo\ to \FinalPositionHi\,mm (width \FinalPositionWidth\,mm). Each value uses seeds 1001/1002 and 500 particles per seed. A fixed 0.2\,mm/mrad grid is extended if a boundary remains above threshold, with a maximum offset of 3; crossing cells are refined once to 0.1 spacing and linearly interpolated. Grid resolution is not a confidence interval for a crossing. The long-turn edge checks use fresh particles and do not redefine the selected 500-turn window.

\section{Stored-beam transparency}
\label{sec:transparency}

The kick of the four-loop magnet is odd in $x$ on the midplane and its near-axis expansion has a leading cubic term, $\Delta x'\simeq c_3x^3$ with $c_3={}$\CubicCoeff\,rad\,m$^{-3}$ at $S=\OpScale$. For an aligned kicker and a centred, symmetric Gaussian beam the ideal centroid kick therefore vanishes exactly. This does not imply unchanged finite-beam moments: deterministic quadrature gives single-pass projected emittance changes of \AlignedMomentX\ horizontally and \AlignedMomentY\ vertically. Orders 20, 40 and \MomentGHOrder\ per axis agree at the reported precision. The meaningful quantities are the response to an offset between kicker and stored orbit and the emittance change, evaluated deterministically from the exact field by Gauss--Hermite quadrature (figure~\ref{fig:transp}, right). Offsets of 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5\,mm give centroid amplitudes $A_x$ of \TranspOneAmp, \TranspTwoAmp\ and \TranspFiveAmp\ of the rms size, and the spread of the kick across the beam increases the emittance about the displaced centroid by \TranspOneEmit, \TranspTwoEmit\ and \TranspFiveEmit; a 0.2\,mm vertical offset gives \TranspVertTwoAmp\ of the vertical rms size. Once the centroid oscillation has also filamented, the rms emittance grows by the factor $1+A_x^2/2+\Delta\varepsilon_x/\varepsilon_x$, i.e.\ \TranspTwoTotal\ at 0.2\,mm and \TranspFiveTotal\ at 0.5\,mm. Native tracking uses two seeds (11 and 12), each with 300 paired particles, with radiation on. The maximum centroid difference is measured at the ends of turns 1--50, and projected emittance at the end of turn \TrackedTurns: $A_x/\sigma_x=\TrackedTwoAmp$ and \TrackedFiveAmp, and mean paired emittance ratios \TrackedTwoEmitRatio\ and \TrackedFiveEmitRatio. These tracked observables need not equal a fully filamented analytic estimate. In this ideal geometry and at the assumed emittance, an offset of 0.2\,mm keeps the stored-beam disturbance at about a tenth of its size, whereas 0.5\,mm is not transparent. The bicubic interpolation of the map agrees with the exact field to \BicubicErr\,rad near the axis, whereas bilinear interpolation errs by \BilinearErr\,rad and overstates the 0.1\,mm response nineteen-fold (\TranspBilinOne). The calibrated dipole control would displace the stored beam by about \CtrlDipoleAmp\ rms sizes in a linear estimate; this hypothetical figure only illustrates why a uniform kicker cannot be used for top-up.

The assumed vertical-to-horizontal emittance ratio is 0.10, as supplied by the author; for a fixed vertical dipole kick, $A_y/\sigma_y\propto\varepsilon_y^{-1/2}$. Individual wire displacements and independent loop-current errors break the cancellation (tables~\ref{tab:wiretol} and \ref{tab:looptol}). The one-error-at-a-time wire limits range from \WireTolMin\ to \WireTolMax\,$\mu$m for both centroid amplitudes to remain below 0.1\,$\sigma$. Independent loop currents require relative balance of about \LoopTolerance. In series powering, a common current error preserves dipole cancellation provided the geometry remains symmetric; geometry errors still matter. These are local linear tolerances, not an allocation for simultaneous errors or a manufacturing specification.

The published baseline thin-septum leakage of about 3.3\,G at the bumped orbit~\cite{Hahn2024}, treated as uniform over 0.4\,m, gives $\Delta x'\simeq\int B_y\,ds/(B\rho)=9.9\,\mu$rad at 4\,GeV ($B\rho=13.34$\,T\,m). The shielded thick-septum value $0.3\pm0.1$\,G over 1.7\,m similarly corresponds to approximately $3.8\pm1.3\,\mu$rad per magnet before accounting for geometry and transport. A local leakage map, shielding and correction assessment are needed before claiming transparency of the entire injection system; the NKM-only cancellation does not supply that evidence.

\section{Pulsed-magnet considerations}
\label{sec:pulse}

The results above apply to an ideal single-pass spatial field. Native tracking of the nominal centroid observes positions \TurnOneXmm, \TurnTwoXmm\ and \TurnThreeXmm\,mm after turns 1--3 at the NKM centre. The corresponding straight-line full-field kicks are \TurnOneKickMrad, \TurnTwoKickMrad\ and \TurnThreeKickMrad\,mrad. Assigning one tenth of the conditional angular-window width (\ResidualAllowMrad\,mrad) as a residual allowance is a design assumption; it gives first/second-return fractional limits \ResidualTurnOne/\ResidualTurnTwo.

For a half-sine of total duration $\tau$, injection at its peak ($t=\tau/2$) gives first-return field fraction $\cos(\pi T_0/\tau)$ if $T_0\leq\tau/2$, and zero otherwise. Later returns replace $T_0$ by $nT_0$ in this finite-support rule. The native-centroid allowance gives $\tau\leq\PeakPhaseMaxPulseUs\,\mu$s; zero field on every return requires $\tau\leq\NoReturnMaxPulseUs\,\mu$s. A 5.3\,$\mu$s peak-phase example meets the latter condition, whereas 8\,$\mu$s retains \EightUsResidual\ of the peak on the first return. Deterministic timing offsets obey $1-\cos(\pi\Delta t/\tau)$, giving \TimingLossMin--\TimingLossMax\ for 1--5\,ns and 5--8\,$\mu$s. The 2\,ns Gaussian rms in the error model is a distribution, not a hard timing bound.

A train with its first bunch at the peak samples less field on later bunches: for the 5.3\,$\mu$s example, 100, 200 and 500\,ns train durations give approximately 0.18, 0.70 and 4.4\,\% variation. At an offset kicker the stored-beam centroid response scales with that field; aligned centroid cancellation does not imply zero finite-beam moment change. On-axis residual dipole and gradient allowances are \DipoleResidualRel\ and \GradResidualRel\ of the corresponding peak quantities. For the assumed flat chamber, a thin conducting layer with half-gap $a=3.8$\,mm has diffusion time of order $\tau_\mathrm{eddy}\simeq\mu_0\sigma a t/2$: a 1\,$\mu$m coating with $\sigma=10^6$\,S/m gives 2.4\,ns and an 8\,$\mu$m titanium coating, as on the ALS ceramic chamber~\cite{Sun2020}, about 46\,ns, both short compared with the 5.3\,$\mu$s pulse; a 1\,mm metallic wall with $\sigma=5\times10^7$\,S/m would give 0.12\,ms and is excluded. For the ALS chamber a 1.1\,\% attenuation of the peak field was reported~\cite{Sun2020}. These are order-of-magnitude scenarios, not predictions for an unspecified chamber. Coating-induced multipoles require chamber-specific analysis~\cite{Kallestrup2019}; no time-dependent capture or operational pulse feasibility is claimed.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Finite-length accuracy is controlled by trajectory displacement relative to the kick-lobe width, not by the label ``kick map''. This complements, rather than contradicts, the small finite-conductor effect reported for the ALS kicker~\cite{Sun2020}: different geometry and launch conditions place that design at a much smaller sagitta, and the result here does not imply that its model was inadequate. The tested sagitta ratio organizes deterministic comparisons over a scaled wire family; it does not establish a universal capture threshold. A sliced vector map retains both the physical vertical field and self-deflection. Local vertical focusing changes sign across the kick extremum. The observed transfer of large horizontal oscillations into vertical losses is consistent with coupled nonlinear ring dynamics, without identifying a unique resonance.

Nominal capture, a joint-error mean and the fraction of realizations above 80\,\% answer different questions. The mean and lower tail depend strongly on the declared corrected-ring population. The 500-turn window is a conditional survival contour; its 4000-turn checks quantify later losses. Ideal BTS steering removes two static coordinates only, while the reduced tuning search uses an ideal capture observable. Neither establishes a practical feedback tolerance or commissioned efficiency. Small BTS Twiss mismatch alone does not establish successful ring capture.

The ring version, aperture shape, IVU assignments, incoming-line clearance, error budget and ten corrected rings limit transfer to hardware. The filament model omits iron, chamber-induced fields, pulse evolution and actual septum leakage. The pulse section supplies consistency estimates, not time-dependent capture. Sextupole optimization may alter the acceptance, as commissioning studies at BESSY~II and SIRIUS illustrate~\cite{Gora2023,Velloso2023}; it is outside this study. The joint capture ensembles vary whole-magnet placement and common field scale, not individual wires or loop-current imbalance. Wire tolerances refer to one error at a time, and aligned centroid symmetry does not eliminate finite-beam moment changes or manufacturing errors.


\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

For the supplied KLS design lattice, the 3-D filament NKM and design booster beam give \ChamberNom\,\% nominal capture with the reference chamber at 500 turns and \FinalLong\,\% at 4000 turns, a late loss of \FinalLateLoss\ percentage points. The interpolated conditional 80\,\% angular-window width is \FinalAngleWidth\,mrad. Finite-length displacement and consistently signed vertical fields require validation, while a sliced vector map provides a controlled comparison within the tested configurations. The final joint-error mean is \FinalNkmMean\,\%, conditional on ten corrected rings; reduced tuning and paired jitter statistics quantify its sensitivity rather than define a universal 80\,\% specification. Ideal aligned centroid cancellation coexists with finite-beam moment changes and micrometre-scale wire tolerances. Pulse and septum estimates further limit any claim of system-level transparency.
