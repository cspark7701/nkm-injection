## Why PRAB is ranked above JINST here

The external clarification places NIM A first, JINST second and PRAB third for a BTS design and optimization study. The revised article supports moving PRAB to second: it develops a physical mechanism beyond beta reduction. Matched optics preserve nominal boundary clearance while phase advance changes jitter transport and blade-loss probability.

That makes PRAB a defensible target. The assumed jitter, model-specific geometry and absence of operational validation still leave a significance question for its editors. This relative editorial-risk assessment is a judgement about the manuscript, not a published acceptance-rate comparison or a prediction of acceptance.

## Recommended submission strategy

For submission with the current framing, choose **NIM A**. If specialist accelerator visibility is the priority and the author accepts the possibility of a novelty-based rejection, **PRAB is a reasonable first attempt**. JINST is the next design-oriented alternative; NST and JKPS remain suitable additional options.

For any target, retain the distinction between model-predicted loss reduction and operational transmission, the scope of the conditional uncertainty interval, the assumed stress-level jitter, the unresolved near-blade discrepancy, and the exclusion of storage-ring capture.

This document records advice. It does not select a journal on the author's behalf or close the deferred R5-BTS-09 submission decisions. The external document's provisional impact factors and quartiles were not used to determine this ranking. No publication-speed or acceptance-probability claim is made.
