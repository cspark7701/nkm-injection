\begin{abstract}
Lower intermediate beta does not necessarily improve transfer-line clearance
when dispersion, trajectory response or an immutable boundary controls the beam.
For a parametric 21.789 m, 4 GeV booster-to-storage-ring line, we derive the fixed
entrance ceiling on minimum five-sigma clearance and compare matched reference,
low-beta and direct-clearance settings. Interior sampling corrects missed magnet
extrema; refined optimization reduces vertical peak beta from 51.27 to 28.44 m.
With restrictions only at the line's ends, where the optics are fixed by the
boundary match, the nominal minimum clearance is analytically independent of the
quadrupole settings. At explicitly hypothetical horizontal and
vertical restrictions, its paired changes are -0.152 and +0.073 mm, while direct
nominal clearance optimization gives +0.881 and +0.212 mm with pointwise 95\%
intervals excluding zero. Station decomposition explains the competition between
beam size and trajectory response. Centred transport and nonlinear Gaussian
quadrature check clearance accuracy below one micrometre in representative cases.
Particle tracking is reported separately: larger mean clearance does not ensure
smaller observed loss counts. These results provide a conditional design criterion
based on the active restriction, without certifying hardware tolerances,
continuous-chamber transmission or storage-ring capture.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} transfer-line optics; physical clearance; dispersion;
quadrupole matching; uncertainty

\section{Introduction}
Diffraction-limited storage rings require control of the distribution delivered
to their injection region~\cite{Hettel2014}. Korea-4GSR provides a relevant
4 GeV design context~\cite{Kim2025,Lee2024}, but a numerically matched exit
ellipse is only one part of transport feasibility. An intermediate beta maximum
is neither a physical aperture nor a trajectory-error tolerance. Dispersion,
centroid displacement, coupling and the location of restrictions also matter.

Published transfer-line studies illustrate this distinction. SLS 2.0 separates
quadrupole-scan beam characterization from position/angle control and identifies
septum restrictions~\cite{Kallestrup2025}. ESRF--EBS rematching includes off-axis
injection-quadrupole fields and mechanical constraints~\cite{ESRF2018}. Sirius
studies distinguish injector stages and incoming beam-size effects~\cite{Resende2022}.
MAX IV pulsed-sextupole injection addresses downstream ring capture~\cite{Leemann2012},
a different boundary from the one considered here. These primary studies do not
establish the particular target or chamber dimensions of our model.

We test when lowering vertical peak beta increases minimum projected clearance,
and compare that strategy with direct nominal clearance optimization at a fixed
exit match. A fixed entrance can cap the minimum regardless of downstream optics;
an internal restriction instead exposes competition between betatron size,
dispersion and trajectory sensitivity. We make that mechanism explicit and test
it in independently seeded machine/shot ensembles. All restricted cases are
parametric demonstrations, not measured chamber specifications. The BTS sequence,
archived match and historical simulations are shared with the companion NKM
study; this article ends at the BTS exit and claims no ring capture or kicker
transparency. Standard optimization is used without algorithmic novelty claims.

\section{Parametric model and physical observables}
\subsection{Boundaries, coordinates and source limitations}
The Accelerator Toolbox (AT)/pyAT model~\cite{Terebilo2001,Rogers2017} contains
36 elements over 21.789 m, from marker m1 to marker m4 downstream of sept\_ex.
It has nine 0.2 m quadrupoles in three triplets, three main bends, an extraction
kicker and two model septa (Fig.~\ref{fig:layout}). Local coordinates are
$(x,p_x,y,p_y,\delta,ct)$ with positions/path length in m, normalized canonical
momenta approximately angles in rad, and $\delta=\Delta p/p_0$ dimensionless.
The six design bend angles are $+7.5,+88.5,-111.701,+176,-111.701,+88.5$ mrad;
reference geometry is held fixed when fields or particle momenta change.

\subsection{Beam covariance and clearance}
The new study uses the booster horizontal design input $\epsilon_x=7.67$ nm rad
and $\sigma_\delta=0.106\%$~\cite{Lee2024}. The vertical input
$\epsilon_y=0.767$ nm rad is an assumed 10\% ratio. Betatron coordinates and
momentum are independent untruncated Gaussians before dispersion is added;
initial transverse coupling is zero and $ct=0$ for this RF-free single pass.
The transverse blocks of the complete covariance are
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_{u,\mathrm{bet}}=\epsilon_u
 \begin{pmatrix}\beta_u&-\alpha_u\\-\alpha_u&(1+\alpha_u^2)/\beta_u\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Sigma_0=\Sigma_{\mathrm{bet}}+\sigma_\delta^2\mathbf d\mathbf d^{\mathsf T},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf d=(D_x,D'_x,D_y,D'_y,1,0)^{\mathsf T}$. Rolls can generate
coupling downstream. Full covariance transport, $\Sigma_s=M_s\Sigma_0M_s^{\mathsf T}$,
retains that coupling and momentum correlation. A local affine map gives the
centroid $\bar{\mathbf z}_s=M_s\bar{\mathbf z}_0+\mathbf b_s$.

For each aperture plane with lower/upper edges $a_-,a_+$, we define
\begin{equation}
 c_u(s)=\min[a_+-\bar u(s),\bar u(s)-a_-]-5\sqrt{\Sigma_{uu}(s)},\qquad
 c_{\min}=\min_{s\in\mathrm{stations},u\in\{x,y\}}c_u(s).
 \label{eq:clearance}
\end{equation}
Five sigma is a declared projected coverage metric, not complete containment of
an unbounded Gaussian. Beta reduction in m must not be called a clearance
increase in m. Historical optics-only ensembles use a separate conservative
100/10 nm rad, 0.11\% beam configuration and are not pooled with this study.

At 4 GeV the rigidity is approximately 13.34256 T m. For $K$ in m$^{-2}$,
$G=K(B\rho)$ in T/m and $B_{\mathrm{pole}}=|G|r$ in T. Assumed bounds are
$|K|\le2.5$ for q11/q33 and $|K|\le3$ for the other quadrupoles. With the
assumed 19.35 mm bore, the respective maxima are 33.356/40.028 T/m and
0.645/0.775 T, below the assumed 1.2 T screening limit. This calculation
checks model consistency, not magnet engineering feasibility.

\subsection{Element-wise restrictions}\label{sec:apertures}
Every element carries its own aperture (Table~\ref{tab:apertures}); magnets are
sliced (four slices per quadrupole, eight per bend or septum) so that interior
envelopes are checked. The only restriction with a source is the injection-septum
blade. In the companion NKM study the injected beam leaves the septum at
$x=-19.478$ mm, with the stored-beam edge at $-16$ mm and a 2 mm blade, i.e.
1.478 mm from the blade's outer face, chosen there as three rms sizes plus 1 mm.
We place the blade at $x=+1.478$ mm along the whole injection septum and at the
line end, on the side facing the stored beam. All other dimensions are declared
assumptions and are swept. Bend chambers are taken as curved: the sagitta of the
reference arc over its chord is \BtsRThreeSagittaBTwoMm\ mm in b2 and
\BtsRThreeSagittaBOneMm\ mm in b1/b3, so a straight chamber would need that much
extra width. Clearances (Eq.~\ref{eq:clearance}) are evaluated at every aperture,
with the projected halfwidth of a round aperture reduced by the other plane's
centroid.

\subsection{Errors, jitter and trajectory correction}\label{sec:steering}
Static machine errors are independent Gaussian quadrupole gradient errors
(0.1\% rms), $x/y$ offsets (100 micrometres), rolls (0.5 mrad) and dipole field
errors (0.05\%). Each shot adds booster jitter at m1: entrance beta factors
(5\%), centroid position 0.5 mm, angle 0.2 mrad and momentum 0.1\%, the latter
carried by the entrance dispersion. These levels are modelling assumptions,
identical to the earlier revision and to the booster jitter of the companion NKM
study; their sensitivity is examined in the results.

Static trajectory errors are corrected as in commissioning. Combined horizontal
and vertical thin correctors are placed before q11, q21, q31 and the injection
septum, and monitors after each quadrupole and at the line end. Each machine's
monitor readings of the zero-input trajectory include a static 50 micrometre rms
error (offset and calibration residual). The kicks minimise the weighted readings
(end monitor weight 3) with the response of the error-free model line,
Tikhonov-regularised and limited to $\pm1$ mrad, in two passes. The correction
uses no knowledge of the actual errors and does not act on shot jitter. Every
machine is evaluated uncorrected and corrected on identical particles.

\section{Physical criterion, validation and comparison protocol}
\subsection{Active restrictions and boundary-fixed clearances}
The two end stations sit where the optics are fixed by boundary data: the entrance
Twiss functions and dispersion are inputs, and every admissible setting matches the
exit to its target. For a centred nominal beam the clearance there is therefore
$c_u=a-5\sqrt{\epsilon_u\beta_u+D_u^2\sigma_\delta^2}$ with the boundary values of
Table~\ref{tab:boundary}, for every quadrupole setting $K$ that satisfies the match.
With $a=19.35$ mm this gives 17.455/18.865 mm at the entrance and 18.555/19.064 mm
at the exit ($x$/$y$), exactly the values found numerically for both settings
in the four-station model (Supplement). The two interior stations have 30 mm halfwidths and could
limit only if a five-sigma size exceeded about 12.5 mm, which no matched setting
approaches. In this four-station model the nominal minimum clearance is thus
analytically independent of $K$: the ``saturation'' of the minimum is a consequence
of placing restrictions only where the optics are fixed, not a finding about the
optics. Under errors, the only $K$-dependent part of the end-station clearances is
the response of the centroid at the exit to errors and jitter. The same holds for
any restriction upstream of q11 or downstream of q33: there the optics are fixed by
the boundary data, so only the trajectory response can depend on $K$.

At a single symmetric active station away from $\bar u=0$, the local sensitivity is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial c_u}{\partial K_j}=
 -\operatorname{sgn}(\bar u)\frac{\partial\bar u}{\partial K_j}
 -\frac{5}{2\sigma_u}\frac{\partial\Sigma_{uu}}{\partial K_j}.
\end{equation}
Switching restrictions or crossing zero centroid makes this expression piecewise.
In an uncoupled plane $\Sigma_{uu}=\epsilon_u\beta_u+D_u^2\sigma_\delta^2$;
our coupled calculation retains the complete covariance. Reducing beta controls
one size contribution, not necessarily dispersion or orbit sensitivity. We export
these variances and centroids separately, adding variances rather than RMS sizes.

\subsection{Accuracy and spatial observation resolution}
Exact independent hard-edge maps are checked against AT tracking at every element
boundary. The original ten-step integrator misses its $2\times10^{-6}$ nominal
maximum-entry tolerance. Twenty/forty/eighty steps give discrepancies
$2.53\times10^{-7}$, $1.58\times10^{-8}$ and $9.89\times10^{-10}$;
new studies use forty. Transverse symplectic discrepancy is $1.22\times10^{-15}$,
without a longitudinal six-dimensional certificate. Translations, rolls and
fixed-geometry dipole-field perturbations are exercised independently. Momentum
errors enter particle $\delta$ without double rescaling magnet fields.

The original affine error maps omit chromatic derivatives about displaced
trajectories. Comparing all 600 original shots with AT Jacobians expanded about
the actual centroid changes station clearances by up to 78.43 micrometres,
but shifts the paired mean by only $-0.155$ micrometres. Eight reference and three
alternative active-station classifications change. The original mean 14.61
micrometres and interval $[-17.91,+48.32]$ become 14.45 and $[-17.88,+48.10]$;
the unresolved-benefit conclusion persists. New comparisons use centred AT maps.
Moment probes are uncensored by aperture limits, so propagated moments describe
the incoming distribution rather than a survivor-biased subset.

Representative simultaneous-error cases compare probe steps $10^{-6}$--$10^{-8}$
and forty/eighty integration steps. Their clearance changes are below
$5\times10^{-6}$ micrometres. Product Gauss--Hermite quadrature at orders three,
five and seven propagates the unrestricted Gaussian through nonlinear AT;
five/seven convergence is negligible on the checked cases. Centred-linear versus
nonlinear-moment discrepancy is at most 0.606 micrometres on four original extreme
or representative shots and \BtsNewMomentError\ micrometres on two new machines
for every regime/design. These finite-case checks are smaller than the reported
effect intervals and illustrative 0.1 mm decision scale; they are not universal
error bounds or Gaussian loss-tail certificates. Intentional scraping verifies
first-loss accounting. Detailed checks are supplied in the supplement/evidence.

Spatial observation is refined independently of integration steps. Exact maps
sample each finite element at 8, 16, 32 and 64 subdivisions, applying bend edges
only once. Original reference boundary peaks 31.44/50.75 m become 31.63/51.27 m;
old low-beta peaks 33.58/28.18 m become 33.77/28.50 m. Reoptimizing vertical peak
beta on sixteen subdivisions converges in 34 SLSQP iterations. The resulting
64-subdivision peaks are 33.79/28.44 m; changes from 32 subdivisions are below
0.00035 m. These are spatially converged sampled maxima, not exact analytical
extrema or a global optimum. q32 remains at its assumed lower bound $-3$ m$^{-2}$.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Active restrictions of the element-wise line}
Figure~\ref{fig:refined} shows the reference and refined low-beta optics, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:profile} the nominal five-sigma clearance at every aperture. The
active restriction is the injection-septum channel at the blade:
\BtsRThreeMinMm\ mm at the septum entrance and \BtsRThreeBladeMm\ mm at its exit,
identical for both settings because the septum lies downstream of q33. The next
restrictions are the extraction septum (\BtsRThreeSeptInYMm\ mm vertically,
\BtsRThreeSeptInXMm\ mm horizontally), again upstream of every quadrupole. The
closest interior restriction is the vertical gap of b2, with \BtsRThreeInteriorRefMm\
and \BtsRThreeInteriorLowMm\ mm for the reference and low-beta settings. Sweeping
the bend half-gap (7.5--12.5 mm) or the extraction-septum halfwidth (5--10 mm)
does not change the minimum; the blade distance sets it directly
(\BtsRThreeBladeOneMm, \BtsRThreeMinMm, \BtsRThreeBladeTwoMm\ and
\BtsRThreeBladeThreeMm\ mm for 1, 1.478, 2 and 3 mm). In nominal terms the
interior optics, including the 45\% lower vertical beta peak, therefore change
only clearances of order 9 mm or more; whatever the quadrupoles can do for the
limiting restriction must act through the response of the trajectory to errors
and jitter.

\subsection{Trajectory correction}\label{sec:steering-results}
Table~\ref{tab:steering} compares the uncorrected and corrected lines on 200
machines with three shots of 2\,000 particles each (seeds 91001--91004); every
setting and steering variant sees the same machines, shots and particles.
Without correction the static errors displace the exit centroid by 1.2 mm rms,
and \BtsRThreeRefUncLostPct\% of the particles are lost, almost all on the blade.
The declared correction layout (correctors before q11, q21, q31 and the septum)
reduces the exit position spread to \BtsRThreeRefCorExitXUm\ micrometres and the
loss to \BtsRThreeRefCorLostPct\%, but its last corrector, 1 m upstream of the
exit, buys position with angle: the exit-angle spread grows from
\BtsRThreeRefUncExitXpUrad\ to \BtsRThreeRefCorExitXpUrad\ microradians. We therefore
amended the layout after this first run, adding a corrector before b3 and a monitor
at the septum entrance so that position and angle at the septum are both measured
and steered; the amendment is recorded in the protocol and the declared layout is
reported alongside. With it the exit spreads are \BtsRThreeRefAmdExitXUm\ micrometres
and \BtsRThreeRefAmdExitXpUrad\ microradians, the loss falls to
\BtsRThreeRefAmdLostPct\% (\BtsRThreeLowAmdLostPct\% for the low-beta setting),
and the rms kick is \BtsRThreeRefAmdKickUrad\ microradians. All later results use
the amended layout.

Two observations matter for the design question. First, after correction the exit
centroid spread, about 0.68 mm and 0.34 mrad, is that of the booster jitter alone,
and it sets the remaining blade loss. Second, the mean
minimum clearance does not rank the variants by loss: the uncorrected line has the
largest mean clearance (\BtsRThreeRefUncClear\ mm against \BtsRThreeRefAmdClear\ mm)
and six times the loss, because a static offset away from the one-sided blade raises
the mean while offsets towards it are lost. The Gaussian loss prediction, by
contrast, agrees with the tracked loss within \BtsRThreePredAgreePct\% (relative) in
every row. The reference and low-beta settings are indistinguishable in every
column.

\section{Discussion and conclusions}
The physical criterion is the active restriction, not beta alone. A fixed
entrance gives an immutable ceiling; an interior restriction exposes dispersion
and trajectory response competing with betatron size. The paired tests provide
three conditional regimes: unresolved baseline change, an adverse low-beta
horizontal outcome, and a beneficial but modest vertical outcome. Direct nominal
clearance settings improve the two restricted mean-clearance cases, while the
particle study demonstrates that mean improvement is not tail reliability.

The nominal phase-space comparison complements these tests: a fixed exit
match produces similar exit clouds even when interior beta profiles differ.
The projected horizontal distribution includes dispersion and energy spread,
so its ellipse area is not a direct measure of betatron emittance.

This adds an observable-specific comparison to established transfer-line
rematching and trajectory-control practice, rather than a new optimizer.
Optimization should state the limiting station, retain full covariance and
centroid response, verify interior extrema, and independently evaluate its
chosen observable. Robust training-based optimization, operational correction
and global search are not demonstrated. Source-confirmed chamber geometry,
matching boundaries and error correlations could change the preferred setting.

All targets, continuous restrictions and hardware limits remain as described
in Section 2. Independent-Gaussian errors and hypothetical restrictions are
parametric assumptions. Numerical precision cannot resolve their physical
uncertainty. This paper neither certifies real BTS transmission nor derives
construction tolerances, downstream capture or NKM transparency. The original
statistical campaign is archived separately; its optical-rule failures are not
particle losses. The resulting design lesson is conditional and testable with
confirmed engineering inputs or future measurements.
