\begin{abstract}
Reducing intermediate beta functions is often used to improve transfer-line
robustness, but its benefit depends on where apertures, dispersion and trajectory
errors limit the beam. We compare two quadrupole settings for a parametric
21.789 m, 4 GeV booster-to-storage-ring transfer line with nine independently
adjustable quadrupoles. Both settings reach the same configured exit optics
within declared tolerances. Constrained minimization reduces the vertical peak
beta from 50.75 m to 28.18 m, yet the nominal minimum five-sigma projected
clearance at four model aperture stations remains 17.45 mm because the fixed
entrance is limiting. On 200 independent held-out static machines with three
independent shots each, the paired mean clearance change is 0.0146 mm with a
95\% machine-cluster bootstrap interval of $-0.0179$ to $+0.0483$ mm; no benefit
is resolved at this precision. Independent hard-edge maps, coupled covariance
propagation and particle tracking validate the local model. No losses are
observed in 1.2 million tracked particles per setting, with nonzero uncertainty
bounds reported. A separate audit retains the historical optical-feasibility
statistics without interpreting them as physical loss. The comparison shows why
beta reduction alone does not establish a clearance gain. The conclusions remain
conditional on configured boundaries, discrete apertures and assumed errors,
rather than measured hardware or storage-ring capture.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} transfer-line optics; beam clearance; quadrupole matching;
coupled transport; uncertainty

\section{Introduction}
Diffraction-limited storage rings require control of the distribution delivered
to their injection region~\cite{Hettel2014}. Korea-4GSR provides a relevant
4 GeV design context~\cite{Kim2025,Lee2024}, but a numerically matched exit
ellipse is only one part of transport feasibility. An intermediate beta maximum
is neither a physical aperture nor a trajectory-error tolerance. Dispersion,
centroid displacement, coupling and the location of restrictions also matter.

Published transfer-line studies illustrate this distinction. SLS 2.0 separates
quadrupole-scan beam characterization from position/angle control and identifies
septum restrictions~\cite{Kallestrup2025}. ESRF--EBS rematching includes off-axis
injection-quadrupole fields and mechanical constraints~\cite{ESRF2018}. Sirius
studies distinguish injector stages and incoming beam-size effects~\cite{Resende2022}.
MAX IV pulsed-sextupole injection addresses downstream ring capture~\cite{Leemann2012},
a different boundary from the one considered here. These primary studies do not
establish the particular target or chamber dimensions of our model.

We ask whether lowering vertical peak beta at a fixed exit match increases
five-sigma projected clearance at the declared model apertures, and whether a
benefit persists under frozen held-out machine errors and independent shot
jitter. The result can be negative: a fixed entrance or a different limiting
observable may prevent beta reduction from improving clearance. We use standard
optimization, without algorithmic novelty claims. The BTS sequence, archived
matched settings and simulation framework are shared with the companion NKM
study. This article ends at the BTS exit; it reports neither nonlinear-kicker
transparency nor storage-ring capture.

\subsection{Beam covariance and clearance}
The new study uses the booster horizontal design input $\epsilon_x=7.67$ nm rad
and $\sigma_\delta=0.106\%$~\cite{Lee2024}. The vertical input
$\epsilon_y=0.767$ nm rad is an assumed 10\% ratio. Betatron coordinates and
momentum are independent untruncated Gaussians before dispersion is added;
initial transverse coupling is zero and $ct=0$ for this RF-free single pass.
The transverse blocks of the complete covariance are
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_{u,\mathrm{bet}}=\epsilon_u
 \begin{pmatrix}\beta_u&-\alpha_u\\-\alpha_u&(1+\alpha_u^2)/\beta_u\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Sigma_0=\Sigma_{\mathrm{bet}}+\sigma_\delta^2\mathbf d\mathbf d^{\mathsf T},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf d=(D_x,D'_x,D_y,D'_y,1,0)^{\mathsf T}$. Rolls can generate
coupling downstream. Full covariance transport, $\Sigma_s=M_s\Sigma_0M_s^{\mathsf T}$,
retains that coupling and momentum correlation. A local affine map gives the
centroid $\bar{\mathbf z}_s=M_s\bar{\mathbf z}_0+\mathbf b_s$.

For each aperture plane with lower/upper edges $a_-,a_+$, we define
\begin{equation}
 c_u(s)=\min[a_+-\bar u(s),\bar u(s)-a_-]-5\sqrt{\Sigma_{uu}(s)},\qquad
 c_{\min}=\min_{s\in\mathrm{stations},u\in\{x,y\}}c_u(s).
 \label{eq:clearance}
\end{equation}
Five sigma is a declared projected coverage metric, not complete containment of
an unbounded Gaussian. Beta reduction in m must not be called a clearance
increase in m. Historical optics-only ensembles use a separate conservative
100/10 nm rad, 0.11\% beam configuration and are not pooled with this study.

At 4 GeV the rigidity is approximately 13.34256 T m. For $K$ in m$^{-2}$,
$G=K(B\rho)$ in T/m and $B_{\mathrm{pole}}=|G|r$ in T. Assumed bounds are
$|K|\le2.5$ for q11/q33 and $|K|\le3$ for the other quadrupoles. With the
assumed 19.35 mm bore, the respective maxima are 33.356/40.028 T/m and
0.645/0.775 T, below the assumed 1.2 T screening limit. This calculation
checks model consistency, not magnet engineering feasibility.

\section{Validation and study design}
\subsection{Independent maps and active error coverage}
We implement exact hard-edge thick-quadrupole and sector-bend maps with edge
focusing, translations, roll rotations and fixed-geometry dipole perturbations,
without AT optics/matrix calls. They are compared with central differences of
AT particle tracking at every element boundary. The declared nominal maximum
absolute map tolerance is $2\times10^{-6}$; transverse symplectic discrepancy
must be below $10^{-10}$. The original 10-step integrator misses the map
criterion ($4.05\times10^{-6}$). Increasing integration to 20, 40 and 80 steps
gives $2.53\times10^{-7}$, $1.58\times10^{-8}$ and $9.89\times10^{-10}$.
New studies use 40 steps; the original miss is retained in the evidence record.
Independent exit beta/dispersion agree within $10^{-5}$ m and $2\times10^{-6}$,
and transverse symplectic discrepancy is $1.22\times10^{-15}$.

Isolated gradient, translation, roll and dipole-field perturbations pass
predeclared local map/orbit checks. Translations drive orbit even when
uncoupled Twiss mismatch is insensitive; rolls generate coupling. Additional
dipole curvature uses AT PolynomB[0], leaving reference bend geometry fixed.
Energy jitter enters particle $\delta$ without also rescaling fields. Tracking
at $\delta=\pm0.001$ differs from the nominal linear horizontal prediction by
about 0.19 micrometres. For 50,000 design-beam particles, tracked/linear RMS
ratios are 0.99896/1.00155, within the declared 2\% check. Intentional scraping
verifies the first-loss element and surviving-coordinate accounting.

These checks validate a local model. Nonlinear chromatic effects are retained
in particle tracking but not the linear clearance estimate; no longitudinal
6D-map symplectic certificate is inferred from a transverse check. Longitudinal
magnet placement, supply quantization and downstream NKM/ring errors are
outside the new error model. Their absence is not evidence of physical tolerance.

\subsection{Matching, alternative design and held-out protocol}
Exit residuals for $(\beta_x,\beta_y,\alpha_x,\alpha_y,D_x,D'_x)$ are scaled by
$(0.05\ \mathrm m,0.05\ \mathrm m,0.01,0.01,0.002\ \mathrm m,0.001)$.
The historical search uses bounded least squares and conditional bounded SLSQP,
then physical post-selection; it does not impose nonlinear beta constraints
during those stages. A replay preserves the nominal start and two random
uniform box starts (seed 42, budget 100). Raw and six-decimal settings are
independently checked against the stated optical scales, family bounds and
positive station clearance.

The alternative minimizes vertical peak beta sampled at element boundaries
with six exact exit-residual equalities, horizontal peak beta $\le60$ m and
identical K bounds. SLSQP converges in 67 iterations from the archived match
(maximum 200, termination tolerance $10^{-8}$). It is a nominal low-beta
strategy, not a direct optimizer of held-out clearance. A separate 60-machine
training audit (seed 71001) precedes evaluation; no test results select or tune
the design. No optical correction or per-shot retuning is performed.

Held-out machines (seed 71002) have independent Gaussian quadrupole relative
gradient errors 0.1\%, x/y offsets 100 micrometres, rolls 0.5 mrad and dipole
relative fields 0.05\%. Each of 200 machines has three independent shots with
5\% beta fluctuations in both planes (alpha fixed), horizontal centroid RMS
0.5 mm, momentum-angle RMS 0.2 mrad and centroid momentum RMS 0.1\%.
These are illustrative errors, not sourced engineering specifications.
Shot covariance and dispersive centroid terms are included explicitly.
Both designs receive the same latent errors and particle draws.

Each shot tracks 2,000 Gaussian particles (seed 71003), yielding 1.2 million
per design. Loss is first nonfinite x after an element; surviving coordinates
must be finite. All element exits are observed and first-loss indices retained.
We estimate paired differences by resampling the 200 machine-average results,
with 10,000 percentile-bootstrap replicates (seed 71004). Particles and shots
sharing a machine are not treated as independent machines.

\subsection{Beta reduction versus physical clearance}
The alternative reduces vertical peak beta from 50.746 to \BtsAltPeakY\ m,
while horizontal peak beta changes from 31.437 to \BtsAltPeakX\ m.
At zero error, both have the same minimum projected station clearance,
\BtsNominalMinimum\ mm, set by the fixed entrance. Thus a 22.57 m beta
reduction produces no nominal minimum-clearance gain under these apertures.

Held-out mean clearances are \BtsNomClear\ mm (reference) and
\BtsAltClear\ mm (alternative). The paired change is \BtsClearMean\ mm,
95\% interval [\BtsClearLo, \BtsClearHi] mm (Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison}).
The interval includes zero, so a clearance benefit is not resolved at this
precision. This is not proof of exact equivalence or universal inferiority of
low-beta matching. It is a conditional negative result for the selected metric,
boundaries, aperture stations and error distributions.

\caption{Left: vertical beta reduction at the fixed target. Right: paired changes
in machine-average minimum five-sigma station clearance for 200 held-out
machines. The bootstrap interval crosses zero despite the beta reduction.}
\label{fig:comparison}\end{figure}

Both designs transmit every tracked particle through the model stations.
There are no first-loss entries in this ensemble; a separate deliberate
scraping validation verifies loss accounting. Zero observations do not imply
zero loss probability. A descriptive particle-binomial calculation gives a
two-sided 95\% upper loss bound of $3.0741\times10^{-6}$ conditional on an
independence assumption. Shared machine/shot effects limit that interpretation.
For independent machine blocks with no losses, the one-sided 95\% upper
probability of a block with any loss is \BtsMachineLossUpper\% (200 blocks).
Observed zero paired transmission change is therefore not quoted as
``100\% $\pm$ 0\%'' certainty. Neither statement addresses unmodelled continuous
restrictions or downstream capture.

\subsection{Separate historical statistical audit}
Reconstruction of all 550,000 historical observations confirms five optical-rule
exceedances, no invalid evaluations, and compatible model/target/error inputs
across four runs. The rule is $\beta_{x,\max}$ or $\beta_{y,\max}>60.01$ m,
or $\mathcal M_x+\mathcal M_y>2.05$, where
$\mathcal M_u=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_{u,t}^{-1}\Sigma_{u,o})-1$
uses unit-emittance transverse matrices. All five failures exceed vertical beta
only (60.23--61.64 m); they are not physical losses.

The descriptive optical-failure estimate is $5/550000=9.09\times10^{-6}$ with
exact asymmetric 95\% limits $[2.95\times10^{-6},2.12\times10^{-5}]$; Wilson
limits are $[3.8831\times10^{-6},2.1283\times10^{-5}]$~\cite{Wilson1927}.
The pooled horizontal median \BtsMedianX\ has the order-statistic interval
[\BtsMedianXLo, \BtsMedianXHi], not a pooled-bootstrap interval. Reconstructed
median, 95th and 99th quantile intervals match the archived analysis.

Three 50,000-sample median-prefix changes (20.830, 11.436 and
10.567)$\times10^{-6}$ miss the $10^{-5}$ target; the 400,000 follow-up
change $2.395\times10^{-6}$ passes. This is median stability, not rare-tail
convergence. The follow-up stopping decision is not archived as predeclared;
fixed-N intervals do not certify sequential coverage. Historical one-at-a-time
sensitivity is mean absolute change of $\mathcal M_x+\mathcal M_y$, not J.
Seed42 ranks beta-only entrance errors (0.00890374), gradients (0.000861122)
and energy (0.000106336). Near-zero alignment/roll mismatch does not certify
alignment/coupling tolerances. Historical and new-model data remain separate.

\section{Discussion and conclusions}
The completed comparison identifies a limitation of beta-only optimization:
when a fixed boundary controls the selected physical clearance metric, lowering
intermediate beta does not necessarily provide a useful margin. Dispersion and
centroid response further prevent converting a beta difference into millimetres
of clearance. The useful design decision is to optimize and validate the actual
limiting observable rather than assume a low-beta setting is better.

This conclusion is deliberately bounded. The target and continuous chamber
are not source-confirmed; four square stations and illustrative independent
Gaussian errors are assumptions. Sampling precision cannot compensate for
these physical uncertainties. The results do not specify construction tolerances,
commissioning feedback or machine reliability. With confirmed chamber geometry,
a different limiting station or target, the preferred design could change.
A more precise paired interval would require more independent machines,
not merely more particles in the same shots. A rare-failure campaign is not
required to support the present unresolved-clearance result.

Independent maps, covariance, isolated perturbations, finite particle transport
and historical reconstruction provide inspectable checks. Nominal matching,
physical station transmission and storage-ring capture remain distinct. A final
engineering application requires validated boundary/chamber specifications and
measurement/calibration information beyond the present parametric study.
