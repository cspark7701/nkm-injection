\begin{abstract}
A booster-to-storage-ring transfer line must transport the extracted booster beam
while satisfying the optical requirements of storage-ring injection. For fixed beam
parameters and prescribed entrance and exit optics, quadrupole rematching preserves
the nominal beam sizes at both ends. We show for a
\SI{21.8}{\metre}, \SI{4}{\giga\electronvolt} line of the Korea Light Source design that the limiting restriction is
the injection-septum blade, \SI{1.48}{\milli\metre} from the injected beam in the geometry of a
companion nonlinear-kicker study, and that its nominal clearance is independent of
the quadrupole settings. What the optics do control is where booster shot jitter
arrives: with matched ends the exit jitter depends only on the horizontal phase
advance, which exchanges position jitter at the blade for angle jitter at the
injection kicker. After trajectory correction, which reduces the loss from \SI{10}{\percent} to
\SI{1.7}{\percent}, this jitter sets the remaining blade loss. A Gaussian loss model agrees with
tracking within \SI{1}{\percent} and serves as a design objective. Matched solutions reach only a
\SI{200}{\degree}--\SI{232}{\degree} phase-advance interval; a setting at its edge cuts the tracked loss on
fresh machines from \SI{2.30}{\percent} to \SI{0.69}{\percent} at \SI{13}{\percent} more exit-angle jitter, whereas lowering the
vertical peak beta by \SI{45}{\percent} gives no resolved change, and the mean minimum clearance
metric cannot resolve either result. A dispersion-free exit is feasible, widens the
reachable interval and lowers the predicted loss a further 65-fold. Aperture
dimensions, including the inherited blade geometry, and the error levels are
declared modeling assumptions.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} transfer line; injection septum; beam loss; betatron phase
advance; booster jitter; trajectory correction

\section{Introduction}
Diffraction-limited storage rings require control of the beam distribution delivered
to the injection region~\cite{Hettel2014}. The booster-to-storage-ring (BTS)
transfer line connects the extracted booster beam to the prescribed injection
conditions of the storage ring. Its optical design must accommodate the incoming
emittance and momentum spread, satisfy the exit lattice functions, and maintain
adequate clearance through the magnets and injection septum. At the septum, the
beam envelope and trajectory must fit within a channel bounded by the blade;
downstream, both position and angle affect the conditions delivered to the
injection kicker. The \SI{4}{\giga\electronvolt} Korea Light Source (KLS)
design~\cite{Kim2025,Lee2024} provides the application considered here, with the
injection geometry specified by a companion nonlinear-kicker magnet (NKM)
study~\cite{ParkNKM2026}.

Previous transfer line studies address optical matching, trajectory stability,
and injection-region constraints through complementary approaches. The SLS 2.0
study separates quadrupole-scan beam characterization from position and angle
control and identifies septum restrictions~\cite{Kallestrup2025}. The ESRF--EBS
transfer-line rematching includes injection-region fields and mechanical
constraints~\cite{ESRF2018}, while the Sirius injection study distinguishes the
injector stages and the effects of incoming beam size~\cite{Resende2022}.
Pulsed-sextupole injection at MAX IV also connects the delivered beam distribution
to storage-ring capture~\cite{Leemann2012}. These studies motivate treating the
nominal optical match, clearance at a physical restriction, and sensitivity to
incoming trajectory fluctuations as separate design criteria. In particular,
a reduction in a beta function is useful for transmission only to the extent
that it improves the beam distribution at a restriction that contributes to loss.

A common transfer-line design objective is to reduce the peak beta functions
and thereby increase aperture margin. For fixed emittances, momentum spread,
and matched entrance and exit lattice functions, however, quadrupole rematching
preserves the nominal beam sizes at the boundaries. Restrictions in the fixed
sections upstream and downstream of the quadrupoles can therefore limit the line
even when its interior envelopes are reduced. The same boundary conditions leave
freedom in the betatron phase advance, which changes the transport of booster
position and angle jitter. A matched setting can consequently change the loss
probability at a septum blade without appreciably changing its nominal clearance.
Distinguishing these mechanisms is necessary to determine whether a proposed
optics improvement addresses the limiting restriction or only redistributes
margin elsewhere in the line.

The goal of this study is to determine how quadrupole rematching affects physical
clearance and beam loss in a BTS line with prescribed endpoint optics, and to
identify a design criterion sensitive to the remaining loss mechanism. The KLS
line is modeled with element-wise apertures and the injection-septum blade
geometry of the companion NKM study. Reference and reduced-beta settings are
compared after static trajectory correction using field trims of existing
dipoles. A Gaussian loss probability derived from the transported beam and
centroid covariance is validated against particle tracking and used to select
a loss-based setting before evaluation on independently seeded machines. The
analysis also examines the reachable phase advance, a dispersion-free exit
target, and sensitivity to the assumed error levels. The study ends at the
transfer-line exit; storage-ring capture and NKM transparency require the
subsequent injection calculation.

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the BTS lattice,
beam covariance, apertures, and trajectory-correction model.
Section~\ref{sec:methods} presents the clearance and loss criteria, matched-end
jitter transport, and numerical validation procedures. Section~\ref{sec:results}
compares the aperture restrictions, corrected trajectories, loss-based setting,
exit targets, and error sensitivities. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the
design implications and limitations.

\section{Parametric model and physical observables}\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Lattice, coordinates, and boundary conditions}
The Accelerator Toolbox (AT)/pyAT model~\cite{Terebilo2001,Rogers2017} contains
36 elements over \SI{21.789}{\metre}, from marker m1 to marker m4 downstream of sept\_ex.
It has nine \SI{0.2}{\metre} quadrupoles in three triplets, three main dipole bending magnets (\texttt{b1}, \texttt{b2}, and \texttt{b3}, in beam order), an extraction
kicker and two model septa (Fig.~\ref{fig:layout}). Quadrupoles are labeled
\texttt{qij}, where \(i\) identifies the triplet and \(j\) the magnet within
that triplet, both in beam order. Thus \texttt{q11} is the first quadrupole in
the first triplet and \texttt{q33} is the last quadrupole in the third triplet.
Local coordinates are
$(x,p_x,y,p_y,\delta,ct)$ with positions/path length in \si{\metre}, normalized canonical
momenta approximately angles in \si{\radian}, and $\delta=\Delta p/p_0$ dimensionless.
The six design bend angles are \SIlist{+7.5;+88.5;-111.701;+176;-111.701;+88.5}{\milli\radian};
reference geometry is held fixed when fields or particle momenta change.

\subsection{Beam covariance and clearance}
The new study uses the booster horizontal design input $\epsilon_x=\SI{7.67}{\nano\metre\radian}$
and $\sigma_\delta=\SI{0.106}{\percent}$~\cite{Lee2024}. The vertical input
$\epsilon_y=\SI{0.767}{\nano\metre\radian}$ is an assumed \SI{10}{\percent} ratio. Betatron coordinates and
momentum are independent untruncated Gaussians before dispersion is added;
initial transverse coupling is zero and $ct=0$ for this RF-free single pass.
The transverse blocks of the complete covariance are
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_{u,\mathrm{bet}}=\epsilon_u
 \begin{pmatrix}\beta_u&-\alpha_u\\-\alpha_u&(1+\alpha_u^2)/\beta_u\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Sigma_0=\Sigma_{\mathrm{bet}}+\sigma_\delta^2\mathbf d\mathbf d^{\mathsf T},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf d=(D_x,D'_x,D_y,D'_y,1,0)^{\mathsf T}$. Rolls can generate
coupling downstream. Full covariance transport, $\Sigma_s=M_s\Sigma_0M_s^{\mathsf T}$,
retains that coupling and momentum correlation. A local affine map gives the
centroid $\bar{\mathbf z}_s=M_s\bar{\mathbf z}_0+\mathbf b_s$.

For each aperture plane with lower/upper edges $a_-,a_+$, we define
\begin{equation}
 c_u(s)=\min[a_+-\bar u(s),\bar u(s)-a_-]-5\sqrt{\Sigma_{uu}(s)},\qquad
 c_{\min}=\min_{s\in\mathrm{stations},u\in\{x,y\}}c_u(s).
 \label{eq:clearance}
\end{equation}
Five sigma is a declared projected coverage metric, not complete containment of
an unbounded Gaussian. Beta reduction in \si{\metre} must not be called a clearance
increase in \si{\metre}. Historical optics-only ensembles use a separate conservative
\SI{100}{\nano\metre\radian}/\SI{10}{\nano\metre\radian}, \SI{0.11}{\percent} beam configuration and are not pooled with this study.

At \SI{4}{\giga\electronvolt} the rigidity is approximately \SI{13.34256}{\tesla\metre}. For $K$ in \si{\per\square\metre},
$G=K(B\rho)$ in \si{\tesla\per\metre} and $B_{\mathrm{pole}}=|G|r$ in \si{\tesla}. Assumed bounds are
$|K|\le\SI{2.5}{\per\square\metre}$ for q11/q33 and $|K|\le\SI{3}{\per\square\metre}$ for the other quadrupoles. With the
assumed \SI{19.35}{\milli\metre} bore, the respective maxima are \SI{33.356}{\tesla\per\metre}/\SI{40.028}{\tesla\per\metre} and
\SI{0.645}{\tesla}/\SI{0.775}{\tesla}, below the assumed \SI{1.2}{\tesla} screening limit. This calculation
checks model consistency, not magnet engineering feasibility.

\subsection{Element-wise restrictions}\label{sec:apertures}
Every element of the BTS line carries its own aperture
(Table~\ref{tab:apertures}); no element is added, removed or subdivided, and AT
checks each aperture at the element entrance. Interior envelopes are sampled for the
nominal profile on an observation-only copy (four subdivisions per quadrupole, eight
per bend or septum), which is never tracked.

The injection-septum blade separates the main-field channel traversed by the
injected beam from the stored-beam region, where a small magnetic field is
required. Its assumed geometry is inherited from the companion NKM
study~\cite{ParkNKM2026}. Let $X$ denote the horizontal coordinate relative to the
storage-ring reference orbit at the septum exit. The blade face toward the stored
beam is at $X=\SI{-16}{\milli\metre}$ and its thickness is \SI{2}{\milli\metre},
so the face toward the injected beam is at $X_b=\SI{-18}{\milli\metre}$.
The nominal injected-beam centroid is at $X_{\rm inj}=\SI{-19.478}{\milli\metre}$,
giving the centroid-to-blade distance
\begin{equation}
 d_b=X_b-X_{\rm inj}=\SI{1.478}{\milli\metre}.
 \label{eq:blade-distance}
\end{equation}
For the configured beam, this distance is approximately three rms horizontal
beam sizes plus \SI{1}{\milli\metre}. Thus $d_b$ is neither the blade thickness nor its distance from the storage-ring
reference orbit. In the local BTS coordinate at the exit, $x=X-X_{\rm inj}$,
the nominal injected beam is centered at $x=0$ and the blade face is at
$x=+d_b$. This boundary is applied on the side facing the stored beam in the
injection septum and at the line end (marker m4). The five-sigma clearance in
Eq.~\ref{eq:clearance} additionally subtracts the projected beam envelope and
includes any centroid displacement; it is distinct from $d_b$.

The injected beam is intentionally deflected by the septum's main magnetic field
while it travels through the injection channel. Proximity to the blade does not
imply that the beam is in a field-free region. In the BTS model, the septum's
bending field is represented by a dipole element, while the blade face is
represented separately by the aperture boundary. Detailed spatial fringe-field
and leakage-field distributions are not resolved. The inherited blade dimensions
and offset are study assumptions rather than independently established hardware
specifications; the remaining aperture dimensions are also assumed and swept.

Bend chambers are taken as curved: the sagitta of the
reference arc over its chord is \SI{\BtsRThreeSagittaBTwoMm}{\milli\metre} in b2 and
\SI{\BtsRThreeSagittaBOneMm}{\milli\metre} in b1/b3, so a straight chamber would need that much
extra width. Clearances (Eq.~\ref{eq:clearance}) are evaluated at every aperture,
with the projected halfwidth of a round aperture reduced by the other plane's
centroid.

\subsection{Errors, jitter and trajectory correction}\label{sec:steering}
Static machine errors are independent Gaussian quadrupole gradient errors
(\SI{0.1}{\percent} rms), $x/y$ offsets (\SI{100}{\micro\metre}), rolls (\SI{0.5}{\milli\radian}) and dipole field
errors (\SI{0.05}{\percent}). Each shot adds booster jitter at m1: entrance beta factors
(\SI{5}{\percent}), centroid position \SI{0.5}{\milli\metre}, angle \SI{0.2}{\milli\radian} and momentum \SI{0.1}{\percent}, the latter
carried by the entrance dispersion. These levels are modeling assumptions,
with the same booster jitter as the companion NKM study~\cite{ParkNKM2026};
their sensitivity is examined in Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}.

Static trajectory errors are corrected without changing the line: no corrector or
monitor element is added. The horizontal knobs are field trims of the six existing
dipoles, applied as additional fixed-geometry
dipole fields like the dipole errors. Horizontal readings are taken at existing
element boundaries, after each quadrupole, at the injection-septum entrance and at
the line end, with a static \SI{50}{\micro\metre} rms reading error (offset and calibration
residual). The trims minimize the weighted readings (septum entrance and end weighted
3) with the response of the error-free model line, Tikhonov-regularized and limited
to $\pm\SI{1}{\milli\radian}$ of kick, in two passes. The correction uses no knowledge of the actual
errors and does not act on shot jitter. The line has no vertical knob, so the vertical
plane is not corrected. Every machine is evaluated uncorrected and corrected on
identical particles.

\section{Physical criterion, validation and comparison protocol}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Active restrictions and boundary-fixed clearances}
The two end stations sit where the optics are fixed by boundary data: the entrance
Courant--Snyder parameters and dispersion are inputs, and every admissible setting matches the
exit to its target. For a centered nominal beam the clearance there is therefore
$c_u=a-5\sqrt{\epsilon_u\beta_u+D_u^2\sigma_\delta^2}$ with the boundary values of
Table~\ref{tab:boundary}, for every quadrupole setting $K$ that satisfies the match.
With $a=\SI{19.35}{\milli\metre}$ this gives \SI{17.455}{\milli\metre}/\SI{18.865}{\milli\metre} at the entrance and \SI{18.555}{\milli\metre}/\SI{19.064}{\milli\metre}
at the exit ($x$/$y$), exactly the values found numerically for both settings
in the four-station model. The two interior stations have \SI{30}{\milli\metre} halfwidths and could
limit only if a five-sigma size exceeded about \SI{12.5}{\milli\metre}, which no matched setting
approaches. In this four-station model the nominal minimum clearance is thus
analytically independent of $K$: the ``saturation'' of the minimum is a consequence
of placing restrictions only where the optics are fixed, not a finding about the
optics. Under errors, the only $K$-dependent part of the end-station clearances is
the response of the centroid at the exit to errors and jitter. The same holds for
any restriction upstream of q11 or downstream of q33: there the optics are fixed by
the boundary data, so only the trajectory response can depend on $K$.

At a single symmetric active station away from $\bar u=0$, the local sensitivity is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial c_u}{\partial K_j}=
 -\operatorname{sgn}(\bar u)\frac{\partial\bar u}{\partial K_j}
 -\frac{5}{2\sigma_u}\frac{\partial\Sigma_{uu}}{\partial K_j}.
\end{equation}
Switching restrictions or crossing zero centroid makes this expression piecewise.
In an uncoupled plane $\Sigma_{uu}=\epsilon_u\beta_u+D_u^2\sigma_\delta^2$;
our coupled calculation retains the complete covariance. Reducing beta controls
one size contribution, not necessarily dispersion or orbit sensitivity. We export
these variances and centroids separately, adding variances rather than RMS sizes.

\subsection{Jitter transfer between matched ends}\label{sec:jitter}
Because both ends are matched, the horizontal transfer matrix from m1 to m4 is
\begin{equation}
 M_x=B_4R(\mu_x)B_1^{-1},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt\beta&0\\-\alpha/\sqrt\beta&1/\sqrt\beta\end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
with $R(\mu_x)$ a rotation by the horizontal phase advance and $B_{1,4}$ the
boundary Courant--Snyder parameters of Table~\ref{tab:boundary}. A shot centroid at m1 with
covariance $J$ (position \SI{0.5}{\milli\metre}, angle \SI{0.2}{\milli\radian}) therefore reaches the exit with
covariance $M_xJM_x^{\mathsf T}$ plus the momentum term $\sigma_{\delta,j}^2\mathbf d_4
\mathbf d_4^{\mathsf T}$, which is the same for every matched setting. The
quadrupoles influence the exit jitter only through $\mu_x$. Over a full period the
exit position spread ranges from \SI{0.26}{\milli\metre} to \SI{0.95}{\milli\metre} and the angle spread from \SI{0.12}{\milli\radian} to
\SI{0.41}{\milli\radian}, in opposite phase (Fig.~\ref{fig:jitter}, left): a matched line can move
booster jitter between position, which the septum blade sees, and angle, which the
injection kicker sees, but cannot remove it. Steered static errors add a small
residual (Section~\ref{sec:steering-results}).

\subsection{Loss-based observable and design}\label{sec:loss}
For a Gaussian projection with centroid $\bar u$ and rms size $\sigma$ at an
aperture with edges $a_\pm$, the lost fraction is
\begin{equation}
 P=\Phi\!\left(\frac{a_--\bar u}{\sigma}\right)+\Phi\!\left(\frac{\bar u-a_+}{\sigma}\right),
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $\Phi$ the standard normal distribution function. Per shot, the line loss is
bracketed by the largest single-aperture $P$ and the union bound; we report the
former as the prediction. When the centroid is itself Gaussian, as for linearly
transported jitter, the shot-averaged lost fraction is Eq.~(\ref{eq:loss}) with the
summed covariance of beam and jitter. This makes a smooth, loss-relevant objective.
The loss-based setting minimizes the logarithm of the largest shot-averaged $P$ on
the error-free line, subject to the six exit equalities, horizontal peak beta
$\le\SI{60}{\metre}$ and the strength bounds, using sequential least-squares programming
(SLSQP)~\cite{Kraft1988} with a 200-iteration budget and termination tolerance
$10^{-10}$. Three initial settings are evaluated in the bounded search. The setting is frozen
before a fresh test on 200 steered machines, evaluated with
tracked particles.

\subsection{Accuracy and spatial observation resolution}
Exact independent hard-edge maps are checked against AT tracking at every element
boundary. Tracking uses 40 integration steps per magnet, with an absolute
nominal-map maximum-entry tolerance of $2\times10^{-6}$ in the stated SI
coordinate convention. Table~\ref{tab:accuracy} summarizes the accuracy checks.
Transverse symplectic discrepancy is $1.22\times10^{-15}$,
without a longitudinal six-dimensional certificate. Translations, rolls and
fixed-geometry dipole-field perturbations are exercised independently. Momentum
errors enter particle $\delta$ without double rescaling magnet fields.

The original affine error maps omit chromatic derivatives about displaced
trajectories. Comparing all 600 original shots with AT Jacobians expanded about
the actual centroid changes station clearances by up to \SI{78.43}{\micro\metre},
but shifts the paired mean by only $\SI{-0.155}{\micro\metre}$. Eight reference and three
alternative active-station classifications change. The original mean \SI{14.61}{\micro\metre} and interval $[-17.91,+48.32]$ become 14.45 and $[-17.88,+48.10]$;
the unresolved-benefit conclusion persists. New comparisons use centered AT maps.
Moment probes are uncensored by aperture limits, so propagated moments describe
the incoming distribution rather than a survivor-biased subset.

Representative simultaneous-error cases compare probe steps $10^{-6}$--$10^{-8}$
and forty/eighty integration steps. Their clearance changes are below
\SI{5e-6}{\micro\metre}. Product Gauss--Hermite quadrature at orders three,
five and seven propagates the unrestricted Gaussian through nonlinear AT;
five/seven convergence is negligible on the checked cases. Centered-linear versus
nonlinear-moment discrepancy is at most \SI{0.606}{\micro\metre} on four original extreme
or representative shots and \SI{\BtsNewMomentError}{\micro\metre} on two new machines
for every regime/design. These finite-case checks are smaller than the reported
effect intervals and illustrative \SI{0.1}{\milli\metre} decision scale; they are not universal
error bounds or Gaussian loss-tail certificates. Intentional scraping verifies
first-loss accounting.

Spatial observation is refined independently of integration steps. Exact maps
sample each finite element at 8, 16, 32 and 64 subdivisions, applying bend edges
only once. Original reference boundary peaks \SI{31.44}{\metre}/\SI{50.75}{\metre} become \SI{31.63}{\metre}/\SI{51.27}{\metre};
old low-beta peaks \SI{33.58}{\metre}/\SI{28.18}{\metre} become \SI{33.77}{\metre}/\SI{28.50}{\metre}. Reoptimizing vertical peak
beta on sixteen subdivisions converges in 34 SLSQP iterations. The resulting
64-subdivision peaks are \SI{33.79}{\metre}/\SI{28.44}{\metre}; changes from 32 subdivisions are below
\SI{0.00035}{\metre}. Table~\ref{tab:spatial-resolution} reports the refined setting at each observation resolution.
These are spatially converged sampled maxima, not exact analytical
extrema or a global optimum. The second quadrupole in the third triplet, \texttt{q32}, remains at its assumed lower bound $\SI{-3}{\per\square\metre}$.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Active restrictions of the element-wise line}
Figure~\ref{fig:refined} shows the reference and refined low-beta optics, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:profile} the nominal five-sigma clearance at every aperture. The
active restriction is the injection-septum channel at the blade:
\SI{\BtsRThreeMinMm}{\milli\metre} at the septum entrance and \SI{\BtsRThreeBladeMm}{\milli\metre} at its exit,
identical for both settings because the septum lies downstream of q33. The next
restrictions are the extraction septum (\SI{\BtsRThreeSeptInYMm}{\milli\metre} vertically,
\SI{\BtsRThreeSeptInXMm}{\milli\metre} horizontally), again upstream of every quadrupole. The
closest interior restriction is the vertical gap of b2, with \SI{\BtsRThreeInteriorRefMm}{\milli\metre}
and \SI{\BtsRThreeInteriorLowMm}{\milli\metre} for the reference and low-beta settings. Sweeping
the bend half-gap (\SI{7.5}{\milli\metre}--\SI{12.5}{\milli\metre}) or the extraction-septum halfwidth (\SI{5}{\milli\metre}--\SI{10}{\milli\metre})
does not change the minimum; the blade distance sets it directly
(\SI{\BtsRThreeBladeOneMm}{\milli\metre}, \SI{\BtsRThreeMinMm}{\milli\metre}, \SI{\BtsRThreeBladeTwoMm}{\milli\metre} and
\SI{\BtsRThreeBladeThreeMm}{\milli\metre} for \SI{1}{\milli\metre}, \SI{1.478}{\milli\metre}, \SI{2}{\milli\metre} and \SI{3}{\milli\metre}). In nominal terms the
interior optics, including the \SI{45}{\percent} lower vertical beta peak, therefore change
only clearances of order \SI{9}{\milli\metre} or more; whatever the quadrupoles can do for the
limiting restriction must act through the response of the trajectory to errors
and jitter.

\subsection{Reachable phase advance and the loss-based setting}\label{sec:design}
Bounded least-squares matching from \BtsRThreePoolStarts\ random starts finds
\BtsRThreePoolSolutions\ matched settings within the strength bounds. Their horizontal
phase advance spans only \SI{\BtsRThreeMuMin}{\degree}--\SI{\BtsRThreeMuMax}{\degree}: the exit match,
with its dispersion targets and the bend layout, pins $\mu_x$ to an interval in
which booster jitter falls largely on the exit position. The reference
(\SI{\BtsRThreeRefMu}{\degree}) and low-beta (\SI{\BtsRThreeLowMu}{\degree}) settings lie near
its middle. Within the interval the predicted shot-averaged blade loss varies by more
than a factor of ten (Fig.~\ref{fig:jitter}, right) and decreases monotonically
towards the lower edge.

is therefore a feasible point at the boundary of the reachable interval, not a
certified optimum. Its predicted shot-averaged loss is \SI{\BtsRThreeRobustShotPct}{\percent}

\subsection{Exit target and dispersion}\label{sec:exit}
At m4 the configured target gives the injected beam an rms divergence of
\SI{\BtsRThreeTotDivUrad}{\micro\radian}: \BtsRThreeBetDivUrad\ from the betatron
emittance and \BtsRThreeDispDivUrad\ from $D'_x\sigma_\delta$. The \SI{0.1}{\percent} shot
energy jitter adds a centroid angle of \SI{\BtsRThreeEJitAngUrad}{\micro\radian} and a
position of \SI{\BtsRThreeEJitPosUm}{\micro\metre} rms. Both terms are fixed by the
target, the same for every matched setting, and absent for a dispersion-free exit.

Matching the same Courant--Snyder parameters with $D_x=D'_x=0$ at m4 is feasible within the
strength bounds: all \BtsRThreeFreeWithin\ solutions from \BtsRThreeFreeStarts\
bounded random starts keep both peak betas below \SI{60}{\metre} (smallest larger peak
\SI{\BtsRThreeFreeMinPeak}{\metre}). The dispersion-free target also widens the reachable
phase advance to \SI{\BtsRThreeFreeMuMin}{\degree}--\SI{\BtsRThreeFreeMuMax}{\degree}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:jitter}), which includes phases where booster jitter falls mainly on
the exit angle. The best of these solutions ($\mu_x=\SI{\BtsRThreeFreeBestMu}{\degree}$,
peak betas \SI{\BtsRThreeFreeBestPeakX}{\metre}/\SI{\BtsRThreeFreeBestPeakY}{\metre}) has a shot-averaged
blade loss of $\BtsRThreeFreeBestLoss$, \BtsRThreeFreeGainFactor\ times below the
loss-based setting with the configured target. Its exit jitter is
\SI{\BtsRThreeFreeBestJxUm}{\micro\metre} and \SI{\BtsRThreeFreeBestJxpUrad}{\micro\radian},
and the beam divergence falls to \SI{\BtsRThreeBetDivUrad}{\micro\radian}. These numbers
are analytic, from the same Gaussian model whose predictions agreed with tracking
within \SI{1}{\percent} above; the dispersion-free settings were not tracked on test machines.

The exit target is thus the BTS design quantity with the largest leverage on
injection. Removing the exit dispersion lowers the injected beam's divergence by a
quarter and lets the line put booster jitter where the septum blade does not see
it, but the angle jitter, \SI{\BtsRThreeFreeBestJxpUrad}{\micro\radian} against
\BtsRThreeRefJxpUrad\ for the reference, then arrives at the injection kicker. In the
companion study~\cite{ParkNKM2026}, the kicker's \SI{80}{\percent} angular acceptance is \SI{0.44}{\milli\radian}, and once the static
vertical injection error left by transfer-line magnet errors is removed, booster position
and angle jitter are the dominant kicker-specific capture losses. This line has no
vertical correction; correcting that vertical error will be addressed in a future study.
Whether the trade favors the blade or the kicker must be decided with the ring, not
within the line.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the assumed error levels}\label{sec:sensitivity}
The \SI{0.5}{\milli\radian} rms quadrupole roll is large for a transfer line, and \SI{0.1}{\percent} rms shot
energy jitter is at the high end of booster performance. Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}
repeats the steered evaluation with rolls of \SI{0.1}{\milli\radian}, \SI{0.2}{\milli\radian} and \SI{0.5}{\milli\radian} and energy jitter
of \SI{0.05}{\percent} and \SI{0.1}{\percent} on another 200 independently seeded machines (analytic loss and
clearance, no particle tracking). The roll level changes the predicted loss by at most
\BtsRThreeRollEffectPct\ percentage points: rolls couple a small part of the
horizontal motion into the vertical plane, whose restrictions keep several
millimeters of clearance, and leave the horizontal blade loss unchanged. Halving the
energy jitter lowers the reference loss from \SI{\BtsRThreeEnergyRefFull}{\percent} to
\SI{\BtsRThreeEnergyRefHalf}{\percent}, because its \SI{81}{\micro\metre} exit contribution is small
compared with the betatron jitter. The ranking of the settings is the same at every
level. On this population the loss-based setting is \BtsRThreeSensRatio\ times below
the reference, against \BtsRThreeLossRatio\ on the tracked test population: the
size of the gain depends on the sampled machines, as expected for a heavy-tailed
loss, while its sign does not.

\section{Discussion and conclusions}\label{sec:discussion}
In a BTS line matched at both ends, the beam size at the ends is fixed and so is the
nominal clearance of every restriction outside the quadrupole section. With the
companion blade geometry the injection septum is that restriction, and it limits the
line by an order of magnitude more than any interior aperture. Lowering the interior
peak beta, the usual design heuristic, therefore changes only clearances of several
millimeters and, on fresh steered machines, leaves the loss unchanged.

The quadrupoles act on the loss through the trajectory. Static errors are removed by
field trims of the existing dipoles, without adding any element to the line; the two
trims nearest the exit steer both position and angle at the septum. What remains
is booster shot jitter, and for matched ends its arrival at the exit depends only on
the horizontal phase advance. The line chooses whether the jitter appears as
position at the blade or as angle at the injection kicker, but it cannot remove it.
For the configured exit, matched solutions reach only a \SI{32}{\degree} interval of phase
advance, and the best setting in it reduces the blade loss 3.3-fold at \SI{13}{\percent} more
angle jitter. A dispersion-free exit widens the interval and lowers the predicted loss
much further, again at the cost of angle jitter. The choice between blade loss and
injection-angle jitter belongs to the injection design as a whole: for the companion
kicker, whose \SI{80}{\percent} angular acceptance is \SI{0.44}{\milli\radian}, exit-angle jitter of \SI{0.33}{\milli\radian}--\SI{0.39}{\milli\radian} is of the same order, so the trade must be evaluated with ring capture, and the
booster's angle stability bounds what either choice can achieve.

Two methodological points carry over. The mean minimum five-sigma clearance
does not rank settings by loss: it rewards static offsets away
from a one-sided restriction and is blind to the jitter tail. A Gaussian loss
probability from the propagated centroid and covariance agrees with tracked losses
within \SI{1}{\percent}, is smooth, and can be optimized directly. The phase-advance view also
gives a quick test for any matched transfer line: compute $\mu_x$, read off where the
booster jitter lands, and compare with the restriction and the kicker acceptance.

The aperture dimensions, including the inherited blade geometry, the choice of dipole trims and
monitor points for steering, and the error levels are declared assumptions; sweeps and a
sensitivity study bound their influence on these conclusions. The loss is heavy-tailed
over machines and its population mean varies between independently seeded
populations, while the ranking of settings does not. The dispersion-free results are
analytic. This paper neither certifies real transmission nor derives construction
tolerances, ring capture, or kicker transparency.
