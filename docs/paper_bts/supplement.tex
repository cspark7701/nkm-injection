\section{Historical optical statistics}
Reconstruction of all 550,000 historical observations confirms five optical-rule
exceedances, no invalid evaluations, and compatible model/target/error inputs
across four runs. The rule is $\beta_{x,\max}$ or $\beta_{y,\max}>\SI{60.01}{\metre}$,
or $\mathcal M_x+\mathcal M_y>2.05$, where
$\mathcal M_u=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_{u,t}^{-1}\Sigma_{u,o})-1$
uses unit-emittance transverse matrices. All five failures exceed vertical beta
only (\SI{60.23}{\metre}--\SI{61.64}{\metre}); they are not physical losses. These historical optics-only ensembles use a separate conservative
\SI{100}{\nano\metre\radian}/\SI{10}{\nano\metre\radian}, \SI{0.11}{\percent} beam configuration and are not pooled with
the article's study.

The descriptive optical-failure estimate is $5/550000=9.09\times10^{-6}$ with
exact asymmetric \SI{95}{\percent} limits $[2.95\times10^{-6},2.12\times10^{-5}]$; Wilson
limits are $[3.8831\times10^{-6},2.1283\times10^{-5}]$~\cite{Wilson1927,Clopper1934}.
The pooled horizontal median \BtsMedianX\ has the order-statistic interval
[\BtsMedianXLo, \BtsMedianXHi], not a pooled-bootstrap interval. Reconstructed
median, 95th and 99th quantile intervals match the archived analysis.

Three 50,000-sample median-prefix changes (20.830, 11.436 and
10.567)$\times10^{-6}$ miss the $10^{-5}$ target; the 400,000 follow-up
change $2.395\times10^{-6}$ passes. This is median stability, not rare-tail
convergence. The follow-up stopping decision is not archived as predeclared;
fixed-N intervals do not certify sequential coverage. Historical one-at-a-time
sensitivity is mean absolute change of $\mathcal M_x+\mathcal M_y$, not J.
Seed42 ranks beta-only entrance errors (0.00890374), gradients (0.000861122)
and energy (0.000106336). Near-zero alignment/roll mismatch does not certify
alignment/coupling tolerances. Historical and new-model data remain separate.

\section{Numerical accuracy and spatial observation resolution}\label{sec:accuracy}
Exact independent hard-edge maps are checked against AT tracking at every element
boundary. Tracking uses 40 integration steps per magnet, with an absolute
nominal-map maximum-entry tolerance of $2\times10^{-6}$ in the stated SI
coordinate convention. Table~\ref{tab:accuracy} summarizes the accuracy checks.
Transverse symplectic discrepancy is $1.22\times10^{-15}$,
without a longitudinal six-dimensional certificate. Translations, rolls and
fixed-geometry dipole-field perturbations are exercised independently. Momentum
errors enter particle $\delta$ without double rescaling magnet fields.

The affine error maps of the earlier four-station campaign omit chromatic
derivatives about displaced trajectories. Comparing all 600 shots of that campaign
with AT Jacobians expanded about
the actual centroid changes station clearances by up to \SI{78.43}{\micro\metre},
but shifts the paired mean by only $\SI{-0.155}{\micro\metre}$. Eight
matched-reference and three alternative active-station classifications change. The
earlier paired mean of \SI{14.61}{\micro\metre} and its interval
$[-17.91,+48.32]$\,\si{\micro\metre} become \SI{14.45}{\micro\metre} and
$[-17.88,+48.10]$\,\si{\micro\metre};
the unresolved-benefit conclusion persists. New comparisons use centered AT maps.
Moment probes are uncensored by aperture limits, so propagated moments describe
the incoming distribution rather than a survivor-biased subset.

Representative simultaneous-error cases compare probe steps $10^{-6}$--$10^{-8}$
and forty/eighty integration steps. Their clearance changes are below
\SI{5e-6}{\micro\metre}. Product Gauss--Hermite quadrature at orders three,
five and seven propagates the unrestricted Gaussian through nonlinear AT;
five/seven convergence is negligible on the checked cases. Centered-linear versus
nonlinear-moment discrepancy is at most \SI{0.606}{\micro\metre} on four extreme
or representative shots of the earlier campaign and \SI{\BtsNewMomentError}{\micro\metre} on two new error realizations
for every regime/design. These finite-case checks are smaller than the reported
effect intervals and illustrative \SI{0.1}{\milli\metre} decision scale; they are not universal
error bounds or Gaussian loss-tail certificates. Intentional scraping verifies
first-loss accounting.

Spatial observation is refined independently of integration steps. Exact maps
sample each finite element at 8, 16, 32 and 64 subdivisions, applying bend edges
only once. Matched-reference boundary peaks \SI{31.44}{\metre}/\SI{50.75}{\metre} become \SI{31.63}{\metre}/\SI{51.27}{\metre};
old low-beta peaks \SI{33.58}{\metre}/\SI{28.18}{\metre} become \SI{33.77}{\metre}/\SI{28.50}{\metre}. Reoptimizing vertical peak
beta on sixteen subdivisions converges in 34 SLSQP iterations. The resulting
64-subdivision peaks are \SI{33.79}{\metre}/\SI{28.44}{\metre}; changes from 32 subdivisions are below
\SI{0.35}{\milli\metre}. Table~\ref{tab:spatial-resolution} reports the refined setting at each observation resolution.
These are spatially converged sampled maxima, not exact analytical
extrema or a global optimum.

\section{Finite loss events and numerical validation}
The new inference export contains exact block binomial intervals and independent
reconstruction of all nine paired clearance intervals. Zero losses among 200
independent three-shot blocks gives one-sided \SI{95}{\percent} upper event probability
$1-0.05^{1/200}=0.014867$. A two-sided descriptive binomial calculation for zero
losses among 1.2 million particles gives upper probability $3.0741\times10^{-6}$;
this requires an independence assumption not justified as unconditional machine
reliability. Exact-binomial attribution is Clopper and Pearson~\cite{Clopper1934}.

The complete source-labelled covariance, linearization about actual shot
centroids, uncensored nonlinear Gaussian quadrature, integration/probe convergence
and spatial meshes are available in the attachment. Quadrature certifies moments
only on checked cases, not rare-tail loss probabilities. Comparison retains
first-loss elements, coupled covariance and exact random seeds. Station
transmission ends at m4; unknown continuous chamber and ring capture remain
outside the model.

The initial shared-aperture hypothetical run is excluded and retained with exact
generating sources. The corrected driver independently detaches the selected
station, verified by a regression test. No numerical failure record is silently
counted as physical success.

\section{Four-station model with hypothetical internal restrictions}
This section presents the four-station study, in which apertures are placed
only at markers m1--m4. In that model the nominal minimum is fixed by the line's
ends (Section 3.1 of the article), so positive results required the hypothetical
internal restrictions below. The article replaces this model by element-wise
apertures; the numbers here are retained for traceability.

\subsection{Frozen strategies and hypothetical restrictions}
All settings share entrance/target optics, quadrupole bounds and beam assumptions.
Six exit residuals are scaled by $(\SI{0.05}{\metre},\SI{0.05}{\metre},0.01,0.01,
\SI{0.002}{\metre},0.001)$. Nominal optimization imposes six residual equalities
and horizontal peak beta $\le\SI{60}{\metre}$. Direct clearance uses a millimeter-scaled
epigraph: maximize $t$ subject to $t\le c_u(s)$ at every station/plane.
Two starts (reference and refined low beta) use 200-iteration budgets and
$10^{-8}$ termination tolerance. All six searches converge; their iterations,
settings, active bounds and independently checked feasibility are supplied.
Exit residuals satisfy declared scales and horizontal screening is checked at
64 subdivisions. Entrance-ceiling ties choose the smallest reference-setting
change; their tiny numerical effects are not interpreted as physical gains.

Three predeclared regimes keep entrance/exit limits unchanged: baseline;
a horizontal \SI{3}{\milli\metre} halfwidth only at m2 ($s=\SI{6.833}{\metre}$); and a vertical \SI{1.5}{\milli\metre}
halfwidth only at m3 ($s=\SI{11.761}{\metre}$). Other limits remain unchanged. These narrow
internal restrictions are deliberate hypothetical stress cases, not surveyed
hardware. A nominal halfwidth sweep of \SI{1.5}{\milli\metre}, \SI{2}{\milli\metre}, \SI{3}{\milli\metre}, \SI{5}{\milli\metre} and \SI{30}{\milli\metre} displays changes
in the active restriction without using test outcomes for design selection.
The legacy lattice shares an aperture object at its internal stations; the new
driver detaches the selected station before editing its limit. An invalid initial
run that inadvertently narrowed both stations is retained separately and excluded.

Settings are frozen before a new independently seeded test. Static errors are
independent Gaussian quadrupole relative gradients \SI{0.1}{\percent}, x/y offsets \SI{100}{\micro\metre}, rolls \SI{0.5}{\milli\radian} and dipole relative fields \SI{0.05}{\percent}. Each of 200
machines has three independent shots with \SI{5}{\percent} entrance beta fluctuations
(alpha fixed), horizontal position RMS \SI{0.5}{\milli\metre}, angle RMS \SI{0.2}{\milli\radian} and centroid
momentum RMS \SI{0.1}{\percent}. These error levels remain modeling assumptions. Each shot has
2,000 untruncated Gaussian particles with the complete design-beam covariance;
all nine regime/strategy combinations receive common errors and particle draws.
No optical correction or per-shot retuning is used. The total is 10.8 million
particle transports, 1.2 million per combination, not 10.8 million machines.

The primary contrast is alternative minus reference in machine-average minimum
five-sigma clearance. Ten thousand paired machine-cluster bootstrap replicates
give pointwise descriptive \SI{95}{\percent} percentile intervals. No simultaneous
coverage claim is made across strategies/regimes. The predeclared useful-effect
scale \SI{0.1}{\milli\metre} is illustrative, not a hardware requirement. First loss is nonfinite
$x$ after an element; all six coordinates of survivors must be finite. Element
exit observations and first-loss indices are retained. Transmission is a separate
observable. No additional samples were added after inspecting outcomes.

\subsection{Entrance and exit phase-space distributions}
Figure~\ref{fig:phase-space} provides a nominal particle-level view of the
configured boundary match. A shared sample of 20\,000 particles from the full
Gaussian entrance covariance is tracked once through each frozen baseline
setting at \SI{4}{\giga\electronvolt}, with 40 integration steps per magnet.
No static machine errors or shot jitter are applied. Observations are made
before the first element at m1 and after the last element at m4; all four
configured aperture stations remain active. A particle is counted as lost if
any exit coordinate is nonfinite. All 20\,000 particles survive in both settings;
this finite nominal demonstration is separate from the error-ensemble loss
study and does not establish real chamber transmission or ring capture.

For these plots, AT canonical momenta are converted to geometric slopes,
$x'=p_x/p_s$ and $y'=p_y/p_s$, where
$p_s=\sqrt{(1+\delta)^2-p_x^2-p_y^2}$ in units of reference momentum.
Horizontal projections include momentum spread and dispersion, rather than
representing betatron emittance alone. The tilted entrance clouds rotate and
change their projected sizes during transport. The reference and refined
low-beta exit clouds are nearly coincident, consistently with their common
boundary match; reducing an interior vertical beta peak does not imply a
smaller vertical beam at the fixed exit. Tracked transverse RMS values agree
with independent linear transport of the same sample to within \SI{0.009}{\percent} in
this nominal case. The clouds illustrate transport and boundary matching,
not a fitted gain in brightness or a new robustness estimate.

No baseline losses occur. For zero of 200 independent blocks, the one-sided
\SI{95}{\percent} upper probability of an observed-loss block is \SI{1.4867}{\percent}~\cite{Clopper1934}.
The event consists of three sampled shots with 2,000 particles per shot; it

The vertical restriction illustrates that a higher mean clearance need not
produce fewer losses: reference/low-beta/direct settings lose 1/43/26 particles
in 1/6/2 blocks (Table~\ref{tab:loss}). These few tail events do not establish a
converged machine reliability ranking. In the stronger horizontal stress case,
