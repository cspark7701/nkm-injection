# Round 2 comparison-table source audit

Checked 2026-10-12 against primary paper text and publisher records. A dash in the manuscript means that a comparable value was not established from these sources. Magnet bore, physical aperture, dynamic aperture, simulated capture and measured injection efficiency have different definitions.

- [Takaki et al. (2010)](https://journals.aps.org/prab/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevSTAB.13.020705), table III: sextupole core length 300 mm, bore diameter 66 mm, integrated field 120 G m at x=15 mm. At 2.5 GeV this corresponds to a 1.44 mrad kick; it is not a field maximum. The table leaves achieved efficiency and normalized disturbance unspecified rather than extracting them from plots.
- [Atkinson et al. (2011)](https://proceedings.jacow.org/IPAC2011/papers/thpo024.pdf), table 1: 280 mm magnetic length, 1 mrad at x=12 mm, 42 x 10 mm aperture. These are the described improved design parameters. The [Gora et al. (2023) publisher abstract](https://proceedings.jacow.org/ipac2023/reference/mopm075-ris/index.html) reports up to 97% efficiency with optimized sextupoles. They are different publication stages, not one simultaneous operating-point specification.
- [Ollier et al. (2023)](https://journals.aps.org/prab/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.26.020101), table IV and sections II, VI–VII: SOLEIL MIK magnetic length 304 mm, nominal kick 2.2 mrad, physical aperture 46.8 x 7.8 mm; injection-point horizontal DA -13/+11 mm. Peak efficiency reached 97% in the bare lattice. The sub-2%-of-rms disturbance uses active compensation. The same paper reports MAX IV's MIK meeting its 10% criterion; other MAX IV fields are left unspecified here. Conductor positioning tolerances were ±10 µm; a cumulative 25 µm displacement reproduced the observed vertical dipole defect, which was actively compensated. This motivates treating ideal geometry separately from achieved transparency.
- [Liu et al. (2016)](https://proceedings.jacow.org/ipac2016/papers/thpmr011.pdf) reports Sirius's 99% design capture over 213 turns and a 3.5 nm rad booster beam. [Velloso et al. (2023)](https://proceedings.jacow.org/ipac2023/pdf/WEPL087.pdf) reports roughly 85% efficiency with about 8% variability at design sextupole settings, compared with the -9 mm design DA. Those measured and simulated quantities remain distinct in the table.

The source PDF screenshot endpoint was unavailable during the audit; tabulated values were checked from the publisher-extracted PDF text. No unverified under-construction system or hardware dimension is added.
