# Round 2 stored-beam transparency

The symmetry statement concerns the ideal centred centroid. The kick has a leading cubic term; a finite centred Gaussian beam still changes its moments. Deterministic Gauss–Hermite orders 20, 40 and 60 per axis give one-pass projected emittance changes of 4.46e-7 horizontally and 1.58e-7 vertically. The assumed vertical emittance is 0.10 of the horizontal emittance. For a fixed vertical dipole kick, normalized amplitude scales as epsilon_y^(-1/2).

The analytic infinite-span vector integral is validated against numerical integration of the closed-filament field. Central differences resolve on-axis dipole and quadrupole changes for each of eight wire positions in both transverse directions and for each of four loop currents. Wire numbering follows the four quadrant loops, inner then outer wire. The full signed two-plane derivative arrays, steps and units are saved in `d9_transparency_resolved.json` under campaign 03. A null first-order tolerance denotes a symmetry-zero derivative below the relative numerical floor 1e-8; it is not an unlimited tolerance at higher orders.

The one-error-at-a-time criterion is a centroid oscillation below 0.1 sigma in both planes. Wire limits range from 0.294 to 2.037 micrometres. Independent-loop relative-current limits are 1.147e-4. Series powering preserves dipole cancellation under a common current error only when the geometry remains symmetric. These are local sensitivities, not simultaneous error allocations or measured hardware tolerances.

Native six-dimensional tracking uses equilibrium Gaussian beams, seeds 11 and 12, 300 particles each, RF/radiation on and one 640-slice NKM passage. Reference and kicked particles are paired. Offsets are 0, 0.2 and 0.5 mm. No particles are lost in any case. The maximum paired centroid difference is observed at the ends of turns 1–50; projected emittance is observed at the end of turn 1000. At 0.2 mm the mean horizontal amplitude is 0.0984 sigma and the emittance ratio is 1.0091. At 0.5 mm they are 1.473 sigma and 2.2407. Finite-sample aligned centroid responses are 3.03e-5 and 5.98e-5 sigma; continuum symmetry does not imply that every finite sample has exactly zero centroid response. No confidence interval is inferred from two seeds.
